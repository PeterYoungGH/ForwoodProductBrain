# QGN 35 – The Integration of Critical Controls into PHMPs for Coal Mines in Queensland - Processing

**Coal Inspectorate – Resources Safety and Health Queensland (RSHQ)**  
**Date:** May 2025  
**Revision:** 3  
**Status:** Processing

## 1.0 Acknowledgements

This Guidance Note is based largely on the International Council on Mining and Metals (ICMM) documents *Health and Safety Critical Control Management – Good Practice Guide* and *Critical Control Management – Implementation Guide*. Adjustments to wording, terminology and processes have been made to better align with the requirements of the Coal Mining Safety and Health Act 1999 (CMSHA) and the Queensland coal mining industry.

This Guidance Note has been reviewed by the Coal Mining Safety and Health Advisory Committee (CMSHAC). Coal mining organisations represented include the Australian Workers Union (AWU), Australian Manufacturing Workers Union, Mining and Energy Union, Electrical Trades Union, Resources Safety and Health Queensland (RSHQ) and the Queensland Resources Council.

---

## 2.0 Purpose

The purpose of this Guidance Note is to provide guidance on how to take reasonable precautions and exercise proper diligence in the development and integration of critical controls into Principal Hazard Management Plans (PHMPs), and their subsequent implementation and maintenance.

Where a coal mine does not have existing critical controls, this Guidance Note should be used to inform the identification, development and integration of critical controls into PHMPs. Where a coal mine already has critical controls, this Guidance Note should be used to review their effectiveness, review compliance with legislative provisions relating to critical controls, and guide appropriate implementation and maintenance.

---

## 3.0 Scope

This Guidance Note applies to persons with obligations under the CMSHA for developing, implementing and maintaining critical controls at Queensland coal mines.

The scope is limited to Principal Hazards where the threshold for a Material Unwanted Event (MUE) is multiple fatalities (more than one fatality). If a Coal Mine Operator (CMO) determines a single fatality to be the MUE threshold, this guidance may still be adopted to assist in managing those hazards.

---

## 4.0 Legislation

Findings from the Queensland Coal Mining Board of Inquiry (2020) and ongoing industry experience demonstrate that controls for incidents with the potential for multiple fatalities have not been effectively implemented or maintained.

This resulted in amendments to the CMSHA through the Resources Safety and Health Legislation Amendment Act 2024. Provisions relating to critical controls commence on 1 June 2025, with a one-year transition period. Mines must comply by 1 June 2026.

---

## 5.0 Steps to Integrate (Implement and Maintain) Critical Controls in PHMPs

The nine steps are:

1. Planning
2. Identify all Principal Hazards and MUEs
3. Identify controls
4. Select the critical controls
5. Define critical control performance
6. Accountability and assigning responsibility
7. Verification
8. Critical control reporting and records
9. Response to inadequate critical control performance

This process is iterative and may require revisiting earlier steps.

---

### 5.1 Step 1: Planning

The Site Senior Executive (SSE) should develop a documented plan covering:

* Organisational context, scope, objectives and timelines
* The MUE threshold defined by the CMO
* Required SHMS amendments
* Consultation and communication with Coal Mine Workers (CMWs)
* Required resources, including subject matter experts and maintenance needs
* Responsibilities and delegations
* Competency and training requirements
* Tools such as BBRA, Bowtie analysis, control identification processes, Critical Control Information Summary Sheets, and auditing and monitoring tools

---

### 5.2 Step 2: Identify All Principal Hazards and MUEs

All Principal Hazards and MUEs at the coal mine must be identified. Prescribed hazards in the CMSHR are not exhaustive, and additional hazards must be identified where relevant.

This step is typically undertaken using a Broad-Brush Risk Assessment (BBRA), producing a list of Principal Hazards and MUEs. Where possible, MUEs should be eliminated through design improvements.

---

### 5.3 Step 3: Identify Controls

Issue-specific risk assessments should be undertaken or reviewed for each Principal Hazard and MUE. The purpose is to understand failure pathways, preventative and mitigative controls, and control effectiveness.

Bowtie Analysis is recommended, though other recognised methodologies may be used. Regardless of method, consequence and likelihood must be assessed.

A Control Identification Decision Tree is provided to differentiate controls from support activities, information sources and verification activities.

---

### 5.4 Step 4: Select the Critical Controls

Critical controls are those necessary to prevent an MUE or mitigate its consequences. A Critical Control Flowchart may assist in selection.

Care must be taken to ensure selected critical controls are genuinely necessary to prevent or mitigate the MUE.

---

### 5.5 Step 5: Define Critical Control Performance

Critical control information must be recorded in the PHMP using a Critical Control Information Summary Sheet (or similar). Each sheet should include:

1. Relevant Principal Hazard(s) or MUE(s)
2. MUE owner
3. Name of the critical control
4. Objectives of the critical control
5. Critical control performance requirements
6. Support activities
7. Verification activities
8. Responsible role and verification frequency
9. Target performance
10. Performance trigger

A performance trigger indicates when the workplace becomes dangerous for the purposes of s273 CMSHA.

---

### 5.6 Step 6: Accountability and Assigning Responsibility

The SSE is responsible for developing and implementing the SHMS, including PHMPs and critical controls.

Roles and responsibilities must be documented, including MUE ownership, auditing, monitoring, effectiveness review and reporting. Management Structures should align with Recognised Standard 22.

---

### 5.7 Step 7: Verification

Verification ensures critical controls are implemented and maintained. It consists of:

* Workplace monitoring
* Critical control auditing

Workplace monitoring is conducted by CMWs and supervisors before and during work. Auditing is a structured evaluation program established by the SSE.

---

### 5.8 Step 8: Critical Control Reporting and Recording

Results of monitoring and audits must be recorded and reported. Audits require review and approval by the MUE Owner, with corrective actions documented.

Audit outcomes must be reviewed by Officers, the CMO and SSE to ensure risks remain acceptable and ALARA.

---

### 5.9 Step 9: Response to Inadequate Critical Control Performance

If a critical control does not meet target performance, a response must be initiated to protect CMWs until the control is restored.

Responses must be approved, documented, and may require withdrawal to a place of safety under s273 CMSHA. Communication to affected CMWs must enable informed decision-making.

## Appendix 1: Critical Control Information Summary Sheet Examples

Worked underground and surface mine examples are provided to illustrate application. These examples are illustrative only and must be adapted to site-specific circumstances.

## Appendix 2: Potential Principal Hazards

Additional hazards identified through industry feedback include:

* Underground: gravity-related structural failure
* Surface: emergency response failure, stored fluids/dams/aquifers, gravity-related structural failure

---

## Appendix 3: Legislation Amendments

Amendments to the CMSHA introduce formal definitions for critical controls and MUEs, officer due diligence obligations, SHMS requirements, and mandatory inclusion of critical controls in PHMPs, effective from 1 June 2025.
