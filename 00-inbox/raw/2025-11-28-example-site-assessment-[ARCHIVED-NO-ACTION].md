# Example Site Assessment — Anonymized Case Study

## 📋 Preface: About This Assessment

This document contains anonymized findings from a comprehensive site assessment conducted across multiple operational locations. Site names, specific geographic details, and client identifiers have been redacted to maintain confidentiality. The assessment methodology, findings, and learnings remain authentic and represent real-world application of Forwood's Critical Risk Management (CRM) approach in partnership with EY.

---

## 🔍 Purpose and Context

The assessment aimed to identify Serious Injury and Fatality (SIF) risks and evaluate critical control effectiveness across multiple operational sites. Forwood Safety, in partnership with EY, conducted a Critical Risk Management (CRM) review to assess how well frontline risk controls were preventing life-threatening events.

The approach did not seek to start from scratch — it aimed to **build on existing safety frameworks and enhance them using Forwood's CRM methodology.** The goal was to reduce risk exposure, strengthen leadership engagement, and shape a resilient safety culture for the next 10–20 years.

## 🚨 Top 10 Serious Injury and Fatality Risks Identified

1. **Vehicle impact on person** — Especially in yards where pedestrians intersect with heavy equipment
2. **Rail impact on person** — Exposure when tracks aren't fully isolated during work
3. **Vehicle collision or rollover** — During inter-site or on-yard driving
4. **Rail crash or collision** — Derailments and railcar impacts
5. **Falls from height** — Unsafe access, platforms, and missing guardrails
6. **Fire and explosion** — From leaking acetylene or propane tanks
7. **Contact with electricity** — Unlocked isolation cabinets and unsafe access to live systems
8. **Uncontrolled release of energy** — Failures in pressure lines or stored energy systems
9. **Falling objects** — Lack of barricades under overhead work
10. **Lifting operations** — Poor exclusion zones and visibility issues

### Critical Observation

**Every identified hazard had low-cost corrective actions available.** The challenge was cultural and procedural, not financial — **leadership visibility and consistent verification were more critical than capital investment.**

## 📊 Field Findings and Failure Data

Across five sites (location-specific names anonymized):

- **141 verifications completed**
- **52 failed critical controls discovered**
- **Failure rates ranged from 20% to 75%**, depending on location
- **Each failure represented a credible pathway to a fatal or serious injury**

The Risk Matrix classified these as **moderate to catastrophic**, with the three leading risks (vehicle, rail, and collision) dominating exposure.

## ⚙️ Systemic Risk Scenarios and Learnings

Each SIF example illustrated how **systemic factors—not single acts—led to critical exposures:**

### 1. Vehicle Impact on Person

**Scenario:** Workers outside vehicles near reversing cranes.

**Fix:**

- Enforce segregation rules
- Implement communication protocols
- Establish and maintain clear exclusion zones

### 2. Rail Impact on Person

**Scenario:** Crews walking across unisolated tracks.

**Fix:**

- Isolate all tracks in work zones, not just those under repair
- Verify isolation before work commences

### 3. Vehicle Collision or Rollover

**Scenario:** Single-point load restraints on freight vehicles.

**Fix:**

- Double restraints
- Proper hooks
- Procedural awareness training

### 4. Uncontrolled Release of Energy

**Scenario:** Propane tanks and acetylene lines exposed or corroded.

**Fix:**

- Install barriers
- Validate containment systems
- Perform regular inspections and maintenance

### 5. Falls from Height and Falling Objects

**Scenario:** Platforms without rails or barricades.

**Fix:**

- Immediate isolation of hazardous areas
- Structural correction
- Install guardrails and establish exclusion zones

## 🧠 Safety Culture and Leadership Lessons

### "Once You See the Risk, You Can't Unsee It"

Forwood emphasizes seeing and acting on SIF risk in real time, embedding the habit of truth-telling and visible leadership.

### No-Blame Culture

The CRM approach promotes a **"search for red," not a search for blame:**

- Risk discovery triggers immediate correction, not punishment
- Eliminating disciplinary action encourages open reporting
- Psychological safety underpins learning and engagement

## 🧩 The Forwood CRM System

### Core Components

**Field Checklists**  
Verify critical controls before high-risk work.

**Real-time Feedback**  
Capture and fix gaps immediately.

**Accident Learning System**  
Automatically updates all clients' CRM questions when a new incident learning occurs.

### Example Case Studies

#### Hot Work Fatality (Desert Case)

Failure to gas test an enclosed space led to a fatal explosion. **Forwood updated the global "hot work" checklist within hours** — all users globally received the change.

#### Warehouse Fatality (UK Case)

A worker was crushed by falling gas pipes. This inspired **new intermodal yard zoning practices globally**.

**These examples show how cross-industry learnings are embedded instantly, preventing recurrence elsewhere.**

## 📈 Key Recommendations

### Primary Actions

1. **Adopt Forwood CRM fully** to make risk visible and manageable across all operations
2. **Remove disciplinary systems** linked to safety violations to foster open reporting and truth-seeking
3. **Implement measurable models** to track safety culture evolution and control effectiveness

### Supporting Actions

- Engage leaders directly in CRM implementation
- Share learnings across all sites and externally
- Measure maturity using leading indicators, not lagging injury data
- Build local ownership — Forwood's model teaches teams to "fish," not rely on external experts

## 🌍 Strategic Vision and Legacy

**Goal:** Make Zero Harm a daily reality.

**Method:** Transform culture from rule compliance to risk ownership.

**Legacy:** Equip every level — from field to executive — to identify and eliminate SIF risk proactively.

**"Add Forwood CRM to your existing safety program and you'll be doing everything you can to reduce serious injury and save lives."**
