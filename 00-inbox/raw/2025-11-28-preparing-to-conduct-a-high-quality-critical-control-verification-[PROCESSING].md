# Preparing to Conduct a High-Quality Critical Control Verification - Processing

_Based on Forwood Safety's video transcript on Critical Risk Management preparatory steps_

## Overview of Critical Risk Management (CRM)

Critical Risk Management (CRM) is the process of identifying critical risks within the workplace and subsequently ensuring that the necessary critical controls are established.

The overarching goal of CRM is the **prevention of fatalities or serious injuries** to personnel while they are conducting their work.

## The Three Preparation Steps

Before a critical control verification is conducted in the field, three preparatory steps must be completed:

1. **Integrate critical controls into your pre-start meeting**
2. **Involve the crew in discussing the critical controls relevant to their job**
3. **Prepare to conduct a high-quality field verification**

## Step 1 & 2: Preparing and Conducting the Pre-Start Meeting

Effective preparation for field verification begins with the setup and running of the pre-start meeting.

### Pre-Start Board Setup

To prepare for the meeting, a **pre-start board must be set up**. This board should:

- List the job tasks planned for the day
- Identify the specific crews assigned to each job
- Align with existing workplace meeting structures while covering key headings

### Active Crew Involvement

Once the pre-start meeting is underway, crews must be actively involved in identifying the critical risks associated with each job. The discussion must focus on the critical controls needed to prevent fatalities, serious injuries, or illness.

#### The Laser Focus Question

To achieve a **laser focus on fatality prevention** (rather than general safety), the facilitator should pose the direct question to the crew:

> **"Is there anything that can kill you while doing this task?"**

If a risk exists that could kill or seriously injure a person, it is paramount to identify both the critical risk and the critical controls that must be implemented.

### Establishing Robust Controls

When establishing critical controls in the workplace, the focus must be on ensuring they are **robust, effective, and expected to work as intended in the event of a failure**.

#### Two Approaches to Identifying Controls

1. **Ideal Scenario:** The work crew selects the risk icons and places them in the appropriate column
2. **Supervisor Facilitation:** The supervisor engages the crew in discussion and places the risk icons accordingly

#### Example: Loading Bay Three

A discussion involving "Loading Bay Three" identified the risk "vehicle impact person." This led the discussion to focus on the necessary use of barricades due to the movement of forklifts—the critical control to prevent harm.

## Step 3: High-Quality Field Verification Preparation

### Selecting the Highest-Risk Job

While supervisors cannot be expected to check every single job before it commences each day, they can select the **highest risk job for the day** and perform a supervisor verification on that specific task.

### Building a Culture of Double Cross-Checks

This systematic approach—which often includes completing a critical control checklist at an agreed time (e.g., 1:00 PM)—**builds a culture of double cross-checks on critical controls**. This ensures controls are verified as effective before the job starts.

### Preparing Questions for Field Verification

To prepare for the field verification itself, supervisors must contemplate the specific questions they will ask the crew to test and confirm that the critical controls they have put in place are robust and effective.

Once these preparations are complete, the supervisor is ready to conduct a high-quality field verification.
