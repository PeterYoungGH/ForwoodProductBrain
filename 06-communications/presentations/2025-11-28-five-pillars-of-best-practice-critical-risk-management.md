## What Is Critical Risk Management?

Critical risk management is a **robust method of barrier-based fatality prevention**. Utilized by leading organizations in high-risk industries around the world, it focuses on reducing or eliminating the critical risks that can cause fatality, serious injury, or other catastrophic events.

Fundamentally, critical risks within an organization's operating environments are identified, assessed, and managed by implementing a robust set of critical controls. These controls are continuously monitored and verified as in place and working as expected before any work commences.

### How CRM Differs from Other Safety Programs

Critical risk management differs from other safety programs (such as Take 5) in a fundamental way:

**Other Approaches:** Workers are tasked with identifying hazards, assessing risk, and developing controls on the spot before starting a job.

**Critical Risk Management:** If there is a credible risk of fatality, workers are told exactly what the hazard is and what controls to implement to protect their lives. These controls are then tested to verify they are in place, effective, and will work as designed in the event of a failure. **If a control is missing or non-compliant, work stops until it is fixed.**

## The Five Pillars of Best Practice Critical Risk Management

The five pillars are crucial steps in reducing and eliminating critical risks from a workplace. To remain effective, a best practice approach should aim for continuous improvement across these five areas, proactively identifying and responding to any new or evolving risk hotspots.

### 1. Identify and Understand Critical Risks

The first step in any critical risk management process is to identify and understand the critical risks that exist in a site, operation, or process.

**A critical risk is a risk with the potential to result in a fatality or life-changing injury.**

Critical risks are prioritized based on:
- Severity
- Likelihood
- Potential impact
- Strategic importance
- Organization and site history
- Past and foreseeable events
- Industry incident data

Forwood's **CRM+** covers the eighteen core risks that can kill across industries and provides a library of non-standard, site-specific risks developed for individual customers.

---

### 2. Implement Effective Critical Controls

Robust and effective critical controls are key to preventing fatalities. However, the ICMM has observed that **most fatal accidents are associated with failures of known controls**—identifying control issues as contributing factors to all member fatalities in 2023.

When commencing a critical risk management program, the aim is to locate where vital controls are:
- Missing
- Not working
- Not complied with

**A critical control prevents or mitigates the potential impacts of a critical risk.**

Critical controls can be physical barriers, processes, or PPE. They must:
- Be designed to prevent or minimize consequences of the specific critical risk
- Be practical to implement and manage
- Fit for purpose
- Be independent from other controls
- Be reliable and repeatable
- Be measurable and auditable
- **Be verified as in place, working, and performing as expected**

Physically checking critical controls is one of the most pivotal steps in best practice critical risk management.

**Best Practice CRM:** A robust verification process specifies what critical risks are present in a task and the controls that should be in place. Workers are protected from new or unfamiliar risks as the process makes clear what can kill them and what controls will keep them safe.

## Forwood's Three-Tier Critical Control Verification Method

In Forwood's three-tiered critical control verification process, critical controls are verified across three levels or "tiers" of an organization via specially designed checklists built into the CRM+ solution. **Every critical control is verified by managers, supervisors, and operators**, with each tier playing a specific role in the critical control verification process.

### The Triple Cross-Check

The Manager, Supervisor, and Operator Critical Control Checklists in CRM+ address different questions around the same critical control to establish a **"triple cross-check."** Cross-checking is a well-established strategy in safety-critical industries such as aviation and healthcare to reduce the likelihood of system failures and increase reliability.

### The Three Tiers

#### Managers

The role of the manager is to:
- Control and analyze each of the risks present at the site
- Ensure appropriate controls are available, including training, design, and implementation
- Create action plans to rectify non-compliance issues
- Ensure everyone participates in daily critical control verifications
- Share lessons learned across the organization

#### Supervisors

Supervisors are responsible for:
- Demonstrating CRM leadership and setting the standard for the team to follow
- Verifying controls are in place and effective
- Completing their version of the Critical Control Verification Checklist

#### Operators

Operators and maintenance staff:
- Implement controls and perform field verifications using critical control checklists each time a work task involves a critical risk
- Know what can potentially kill them on a site or job
- Know what critical controls should be implemented to protect them
- Verify if those critical controls are present and working

**If a control is missing or not working, the work must stop.** The operator must fix the control if possible or escalate to their supervisor or manager.

### 4. Monitor Effectiveness of Critical Controls

In best practice critical risk management, every critical control is actively monitored to ensure it remains effective and to identify instances of non-compliance in the verification process.

Managers are responsible for evaluating critical controls and ensuring the appropriate controls are available and compliant for each critical risk. Using the Forwood CRM+ solution, managers can efficiently evaluate controls by scheduling and conducting:

- **Desktop-based verifications** that validate administrative conformance to prescribed standards
- **Field-based verifications** that validate compliance with organizational processes and procedures

#### CRM+ Critical Control Verification Checklist for Managers

Managers verify three key dimensions:

**Design:**  
Review procedures relating to the critical control, focusing on the engineering standards and regulations that have been considered and applied to effectively manage the risk through the choice of control(s) put in place.

**Implementation:**  
Examine how the control is being used and maintained by operators in the field.

**Training and Competency:**  
Ensure all personnel have the required knowledge and training to competently implement the control and manage the risk.

**Non-compliance in any of these areas increases the risk of the critical control failing and a fatality occurring.**

### 5. Leverage Data for Continuous Improvement

Above all, best practice critical risk management is distinguished by its focus on **continuous improvement**. A data-driven approach enables the identification of new or evolving risk hotspots to proactively prevent incidents before they occur.

#### Leveraging Data for Evidence-Based Decision Making

Leveraging data is a powerful strategy for revealing the safety gaps in an organization. In the CRM+ solution, leading indicator data collected from verification activities can be analyzed to:

- Identify growing incidences of non-conformance with critical controls
- Focus on critical controls with upward trends
- Act in the location where fatalities are most likely
- Benchmark safety performance against multiple sites and industry peers from a global dataset of over 200 million data points

By utilizing these vital data insights, organizations are empowered to make evidence-based decisions and efficiently assess risks, build controls, and implement strategies to address safety issues as they arise.
