# Opportunity: Enterprise Customers Need Compliance-First Product Features

**Status:** Active  
**Created:** 2025-12-04  
**Last Updated:** 2026-01-08  
**Priority:** High

## Overview

Enterprise customers in regulated industries need compliance capabilities built directly into the product that enable them to meet regulatory requirements, rather than treating compliance as a late-stage requirement or legal checkbox. These capabilities must be visible and user-facing to serve as procurement enablers.

## Customer Problem

Enterprise customers, especially in regulated industries like mining, utilities, construction, healthcare, and financial services, face increasing pressure from regulatory frameworks on sustainability, labor transparency, AI risk mitigation, and data governance. These industries are passing these expectations upstream to their tech vendors.

Procurement teams are embedding requirements such as:
- Emissions traceability for infrastructure-heavy platforms
- Accessibility documentation and audit trails for public sector contracts
- Data localization and sovereignty guarantees in line with EU and APAC regulations
- Explainability standards for embedded AI features, tied to the EU AI Act and equivalents

These are not just paperwork requirements. They shape RFP inclusion, renewal decisions, and budget approvals. Customers need products that **enable** them to comply with regulations through the platform itself, rather than forcing them to solve compliance issues separately.

The problem manifests as:
- Sales-qualified opportunities dropping without explanation, especially in EU markets
- Procurement teams pushing for third-party risk assessments that product architecture wasn't built to support
- Higher churn from enterprise accounts whose internal compliance policies outpace product roadmap
- Missed eligibility for public-sector or ESG-linked budgets

Customers are no longer asking "are you compliant?" - they're asking "what does your product enable us to comply with?"

## Insights

Key insights that led to identifying this opportunity:
- Compliance is shifting from a late-stage requirement to a core product capability that enables customer success
- Buyers need visible, user-facing compliance controls - if customers can't see it, it doesn't help in procurement
- The software landscape is dividing into platforms built for the regulated future versus everything else
- Examples of compliance-first features include: sustainability modules, built-in accessibility testing, automated audit logs, and localization controls
- The risk of not building compliance capabilities is "exclusion before conversation" - losing deals before they even start
