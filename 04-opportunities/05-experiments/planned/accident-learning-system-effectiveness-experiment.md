# Experiment: Accident Learning System Effectiveness and Adoption

**Status:** Planned  
**Created:** 2026-01-03  
**Last Updated:** 2026-01-03

## Overview

Validate that the Accident Learning System (ALS) effectively prevents repeat incidents and that users adopt updated checklists automatically, ensuring the system creates a continuous learning loop that improves safety over time.

## Related Solution

Link to the solution this experiment tests:
- [Accident Learning System (ALS)](../04-solutions/active/accident-learning-system.md)

## Hypothesis

We believe that the Accident Learning System's real-time checklist updates after incidents will result in improved safety outcomes and automatic user adoption of updated checklists. We will know this is true when:
- Updated checklists are automatically adopted by users (≥90% adoption rate)
- No repeat incidents occur for incidents where ALS identified and updated missing questions
- Users report confidence that updated checklists prevent similar incidents (≥80% agree)
- System reduces time from incident to prevention measure from months/years to immediate

## Methodology

### Experiment Type
Retrospective Analysis and User Survey

### Approach

1. **Retrospective Analysis (Week 1-2)**
   - Analyze historical incidents where ALS identified and updated missing questions
   - Track adoption of updated checklists across all deployments
   - Measure time from incident to checklist update deployment
   - Identify any repeat incidents for incidents where ALS updated checklists
   - Review Kinross incident case study and similar incidents

2. **User Survey (Week 2-3)**
   - Survey users who have encountered updated checklists from ALS
   - Questions:
     - Did you notice the checklist was updated after an incident?
     - Do you understand why the checklist was updated?
     - Do you have confidence that the updated checklist prevents similar incidents?
     - Did you automatically adopt the updated checklist?
   - Collect qualitative feedback on ALS effectiveness

3. **Safety Outcome Analysis (Week 3-4)**
   - Compare safety outcomes before and after ALS implementation
   - Measure reduction in repeat incidents
   - Analyze time-to-prevention improvement
   - Document case studies of successful incident prevention

### Participants/Data
- **Historical Incidents:** All incidents where ALS identified and updated missing questions
- **Users:** 50-100 users who have encountered ALS-updated checklists
- **Selection Criteria:** 
  - Users from deployments where ALS has updated checklists
  - Diverse roles (operators, supervisors, HSE managers)

## Success Criteria

### Primary Success Criteria
- **Checklist Adoption:** ≥90% of users automatically adopt updated checklists
- **Repeat Incident Prevention:** Zero repeat incidents for incidents where ALS identified and updated missing questions
- **User Confidence:** ≥80% of users agree that updated checklists prevent similar incidents
- **Time-to-Prevention:** System reduces time from incident to prevention measure from months/years to immediate (<24 hours)

### Secondary Success Criteria
- Users understand why checklists were updated
- Safety outcomes improve after ALS implementation
- System creates continuous learning loop
