## Key Findings

### Finding 1: Forwood's Historical Leadership in CRM Development
Forwood is a pioneer of best practice critical risk management, having developed the global standard from the learnings of real workplace fatalities. Key historical moments include:
- **2010–2012:** Built the first enterprise-grade Critical Control Verification System for BHP at the Escondida mine, Chile
- **2012–2014:** Expanded the system to all BHP copper operations in Chile and base metal operations globally
- **2014:** ICMM created the "Health and Safety Critical Control Management: Good Practice Guide" based on Forwood's Escondida system
- **Today:** CRM+ is implemented in over 800 client sites globally

This history demonstrates Forwood's foundational role in establishing CRM as a global standard.

### Finding 2: The Five Pillars Framework
The five pillars provide a comprehensive, structured approach to critical risk management:
1. **Identify and Understand Critical Risks** - Prioritize based on severity, likelihood, impact, strategic importance, history, and industry data
2. **Implement Effective Critical Controls** - Controls must be purpose-built, independent, reliable, repeatable, measurable, and verified
3. **Verify Critical Controls Before Starting Work** - Physical verification is pivotal; digital solutions are best practice
4. **Monitor Effectiveness of Critical Controls** - Managers evaluate Design, Implementation, and Training dimensions
5. **Leverage Data for Continuous Improvement** - Use leading indicator data to identify trends and benchmark against 200M+ data points

### Finding 3: Three-Tier Verification Method (Triple Cross-Check)
Forwood's patented three-tier critical control verification method creates a "triple cross-check" system:
- **Managers:** Control and analyze risks, ensure controls available (training, design, implementation), create action plans, ensure participation, share learnings
- **Supervisors:** Demonstrate CRM leadership, verify controls in place and effective, complete supervisor checklist
- **Operators:** Implement controls, perform field verifications, know what can kill them, verify controls present and working, stop work if control missing

This triple cross-check approach is modeled after safety-critical industries like aviation and healthcare to reduce system failures and increase reliability.

### Finding 4: Most Fatal Accidents Associated with Known Control Failures
The ICMM has observed that **most fatal accidents are associated with failures of known controls**—identifying control issues as contributing factors to all member fatalities in 2023. This finding emphasizes the importance of verification and monitoring, not just identification and implementation of controls.

### Finding 5: Digital Solutions Are Best Practice
Implementing a digitized risk management solution is the best practice method for collecting, organizing, and storing vital safety data. Digital solutions:
- Reduce human error risk and "tick and flick" mentality
- Improve overall data quality for auditing, reporting, and compliance
- Enable real-time updates and insights into emerging risk situations
- Support geo-located verifications to ensure field authenticity
- Enable benchmarking and predictive forecasting using large datasets

### Finding 6: CRM Differs Fundamentally from Other Safety Programs
Critical risk management differs from other safety programs (such as Take 5) in a fundamental way:
- **Other Approaches:** Workers identify hazards, assess risk, and develop controls on the spot
- **CRM:** Workers are told exactly what the hazard is and what controls to implement. Controls are verified as in place, effective, and will work as designed. **If a control is missing or non-compliant, work stops until it is fixed.**

This prescriptive, verified approach protects workers from new or unfamiliar risks.
