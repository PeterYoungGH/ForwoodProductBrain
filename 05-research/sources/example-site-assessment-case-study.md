## Summary

This research captures anonymized findings from a comprehensive site assessment conducted across multiple operational locations in partnership with EY. The assessment identified Serious Injury and Fatality (SIF) risks and evaluated critical control effectiveness, revealing 52 failed critical controls across 141 verifications with failure rates ranging from 20% to 75%. The findings demonstrate systemic risk factors, cultural challenges, and the critical importance of leadership visibility and consistent verification over capital investment.

- Assessment conducted across five sites with 141 verifications completed and 52 failed critical controls discovered

## Key Findings

### Finding 1: High Failure Rates Despite Existing Frameworks
Across five sites, 141 verifications were completed with 52 failed critical controls discovered. Failure rates ranged from 20% to 75% depending on location, with each failure representing a credible pathway to a fatal or serious injury. The Risk Matrix classified these as moderate to catastrophic, with the three leading risks (vehicle, rail, and collision) dominating exposure. This demonstrates that even with existing safety frameworks, critical controls can fail at alarming rates without proper verification and cultural commitment.

### Finding 2: Systemic Factors Drive Critical Exposures
Each SIF example illustrated how systemic factors—not single acts—led to critical exposures. The assessment identified five key systemic risk scenarios:
1. Vehicle Impact on Person (workers outside vehicles near reversing cranes)
2. Rail Impact on Person (crews walking across unisolated tracks)
3. Vehicle Collision or Rollover (single-point load restraints)
4. Uncontrolled Release of Energy (exposed propane tanks and acetylene lines)
5. Falls from Height and Falling Objects (platforms without rails or barricades)

Each scenario had clear, low-cost fixes available, indicating the problem was procedural and cultural, not technical or financial.

### Finding 3: Cultural and Procedural Challenges Outweigh Financial Constraints
The critical observation from the assessment was that every identified hazard had low-cost corrective actions available. The challenge was cultural and procedural, not financial. Leadership visibility and consistent verification were more critical than capital investment. This finding has significant implications for how organizations should approach safety improvement—focusing on culture, leadership engagement, and verification processes rather than expensive capital projects.

### Finding 4: No-Blame Culture Enables Risk Discovery
The CRM approach promotes a "search for red, not a search for blame" culture where:
- Risk discovery triggers immediate correction, not punishment
- Eliminating disciplinary action encourages open reporting
- Psychological safety underpins learning and engagement

This cultural shift is essential for effective risk management, as it enables workers to report issues without fear of reprisal.

### Finding 5: Real-Time Learning and Global Updates
The assessment highlighted Forwood's Accident Learning System, which automatically updates all clients' CRM questions when a new incident learning occurs. Examples include:
- Hot Work Fatality (Desert Case): Forwood updated the global "hot work" checklist within hours after a failure to gas test led to a fatal explosion
- Warehouse Fatality (UK Case): A worker crushed by falling gas pipes inspired new intermodal yard zoning practices globally

These examples show how cross-industry learnings are embedded instantly, preventing recurrence elsewhere.

## Implications

### For Product
- Product features must support real-time verification and feedback loops
- The 20-75% failure rate range suggests product should help identify and prioritize sites with highest failure rates
- Product should enable "search for red" culture by making risk discovery visible and actionable without blame
- Accident Learning System demonstrates the value of global, instant updates—product should support this capability
- Product should help organizations measure safety culture evolution and control effectiveness using leading indicators

### For Strategy
- Strategy should emphasize cultural transformation over capital investment
- Leadership engagement and visibility are critical success factors—strategy should include leadership enablement
- The assessment demonstrates the value of partnerships (EY) for credibility and reach
- Strategy should leverage real-world case studies like this to demonstrate CRM effectiveness
- Focus on leading indicators (verification rates, control effectiveness) rather than lagging injury data

### For Market
- This case study provides powerful evidence of CRM effectiveness and common failure patterns
- The finding that low-cost fixes are available but cultural change is needed positions Forwood as a methodology and culture partner, not just a technology vendor
- The 20-75% failure rate range across sites suggests significant market opportunity for improvement
- Partnership with EY demonstrates credibility and ability to work with global consultancies
- The anonymized nature allows this to be used as a sales and marketing asset
