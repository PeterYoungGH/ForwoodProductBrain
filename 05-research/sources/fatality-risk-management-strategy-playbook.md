## Summary

This research captures a strategic playbook for implementing Critical Risk Management (CRM) in construction and utility industries. The playbook outlines five key strategies: (1) Know Your Critical Risks, (2) Implement Effective Critical Controls, (3) Verify and Monitor Critical Controls, (4) Sustain Your CRM Program, and (5) Don't Reinvent the Wheel. It addresses the global fatality problem (350,000 workplace accident deaths annually, 2 million work-related disease deaths), explains why traditional safety approaches fail, and provides evidence-based strategies for fatality prevention. The document emphasizes targeting the "Fatality Zone" rather than spreading resources thinly, and highlights research showing 80% reduction in high-potential incidents when CRM programs are implemented.

## Key Findings

### Finding 1: The Global Fatality Problem
Every year, approximately **350,000 people die from workplace accidents** and nearly **2 million die from work-related diseases.** These numbers represent individual human lives lost and the devastation of families and loved ones. This scale of the problem creates urgency for effective fatality prevention strategies and positions CRM as addressing a critical global need.

### Finding 2: Traditional Safety Approaches Fail
Traditional safety approaches overly rely on injury statistics rather than proactively identifying and controlling risks. Many common safety programs focus on reducing minor hazards, removing near-misses, and modifying unsafe behaviors in the hope this will lead to a reduction in serious incidents and fatalities. **Billions of dollars are invested in these initiatives, yet workplace fatalities continue to occur.** This finding demonstrates the need for a fundamentally different approach focused on critical risks.

### Finding 3: CRM Effectiveness Evidence
A recent study highlights how the construction industry continues to experience high fatality rates due to an over-reliance on lagging indicators and ineffective control implementation. When a Critical Risk Management (CRM) program was introduced to proactively identify and verify critical controls, the research found:
- **Hazard reporting improved by 8%**
- **High-potential incidents reduced by 80%**

_Reference: Selleck, R. J. (2023). A critical control approach to preventing fatalities in construction. Edith Cowan University._

This evidence demonstrates CRM's effectiveness and provides quantitative support for the methodology.

### Finding 4: Target the Fatality Zone
Instead of spreading resources thinly, CRM targets the **Fatality Zone**—the small subset of events, also called "high-potential incidents," with a credible risk of fatality. By targeting events that are truly life-threatening, organizations can:
- Identify critical risks
- Implement effective critical controls
- Efficiently allocate resources to directly prevent serious injury or death

This focused approach is more effective than broad safety programs that don't prioritize critical risks.

### Finding 5: Fall from Height as Critical Risk Example
Tragically, around **57,000 construction and utilities workers lose their lives from falls every year.** Over the past 5 years, most fatalities in these industries were caused by falls from:
- Buildings and other structures
- Ladders
- Trucks, semi-trailers, and lorries
- Openings in floors, walls, or ceilings
- Scaffolding
- Steps and stairways
- Power hoists

**We know how to work safely at heights, so why do these tragedies keep happening? Because this critical risk is not being adequately controlled.** This example demonstrates that knowledge exists but implementation and verification are the challenges.

### Finding 6: Multiple Control Failures Lead to Fatalities
Workplace fatalities are often due to **multiple control failures** across three dimensions:
- **Design Flaws:** Missing anchor points, inadequate structural considerations
- **Organizational Issues:** Inadequate training, unclear procedures, poor communication
- **Unsafe Working Conditions:** Fear of stopping work due to production/time pressures, inadequate resources, environmental factors

When failures such as these align, the likelihood of a fatality significantly increases. This finding emphasizes the importance of comprehensive verification across all dimensions.

### Finding 7: Three Fundamental Principles
Critical Risk Management is based on three fundamental principles:
1. **Identify and understand critical risks**
2. **Implement and maintain effective critical controls**
3. **Rigorously verify and monitor control effectiveness**

These principles form the foundation of the CRM approach and should guide all implementation efforts.

### Finding 8: Sustaining CRM Requires Data-Driven Strategies
Maintaining a successful CRM program requires disciplined verification, strong leadership accountability, and consistent frontline engagement. Key strategies include:
- Identify who in the organization will be accountable for each critical risk
- Embed Critical Risk Management in company Objectives and Key Results
- Operationalize critical control ownership and link assurance to in-field verification
- Monitor critical controls using a comprehensive digital solution
- Perform regular audits to ensure ongoing critical controls effectiveness
- Measure success with leading and lagging safety metrics aligned to maturity
- Benchmark performance against other organizations in your industry

### Finding 9: Forwood's Global Learning Ecosystem
**Forwood's Critical Risk Management Ecosystem is built from real-world learnings.** By working collaboratively with clients that span 800+ sites in over 60 countries, Forwood has accumulated over 300 million data points informing what good safety performance looks like. This scale provides significant credibility and demonstrates proven effectiveness.
