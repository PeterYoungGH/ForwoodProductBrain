# CRM Operational Pillars

The five operational pillars that guide how we implement and operate Critical Risk Management (CRM) in practice.

## Introduction

While the [Five Pillars of Best Practice Critical Risk Management](./five-pillars-of-best-practice-critical-risk-management.md) describe the methodology and process framework for CRM, these operational pillars represent the core principles that guide how we implement and operate CRM in practice.

These pillars ensure that our CRM implementation remains focused, effective, and aligned with our mission to eliminate Serious Injuries and Fatalities (SIFs).

## The Five CRM Operational Pillars

### 1. Focus on Fatal Risks

**Principle:** Identify only the risks that can cause serious injury or death.

**Why it matters:**  
Not all risks are equal. By focusing exclusively on fatal and serious injury risks, we ensure that resources, attention, and controls are directed where they matter most. This principle prevents dilution of effort across lower-severity risks and maintains clear focus on what can kill.

**In practice:**
- Risk assessments prioritize severity over likelihood
- Critical risk registers contain only SIF-potential risks
- Controls are designed specifically for fatal risk prevention
- Resources are allocated to the highest-severity risks first

### 2. Define Critical Controls

**Principle:** Establish non-negotiable controls that must never fail.

**Why it matters:**  
Critical controls are the last line of defense against fatal risks. They must be clearly defined, understood, and treated as non-negotiable. When a critical control fails, the risk of fatality becomes real.

**In practice:**
- Critical controls are explicitly defined for each critical risk
- Controls are designed to be independent, reliable, and verifiable
- Control failure is treated as a serious event requiring immediate response
- Controls are documented and communicated clearly to all relevant personnel

### 3. Verify in the Field

**Principle:** Conduct real-time checks with frontline engagement.

**Why it matters:**  
Controls that exist only on paper don't prevent fatalities. Physical verification in the field, with active engagement from frontline workers, ensures that controls are actually in place and functioning before work begins.

**In practice:**
- Physical verification occurs before work starts
- Frontline workers actively participate in verification
- Verification is documented in real-time using digital tools
- Three-tier verification (Manager, Supervisor, Operator) creates triple cross-check
- Verification failures stop work until controls are restored

### 4. Ensure Data Transparency

**Principle:** Make performance visible to drive accountability.

**Why it matters:**  
What gets measured gets managed. Transparent data about control performance, verification rates, and risk trends enables accountability at all levels and drives continuous improvement.

**In practice:**
- Real-time dashboards show control performance and verification status
- Data is visible to operators, supervisors, and managers
- Performance metrics are tracked and reported regularly
- Trends and patterns are identified and communicated
- Accountability is clear at each organizational level

### 5. Learn and Prevent

**Principle:** Use trends and lessons to drive proactive improvement.

**Why it matters:**  
Preventing fatalities requires learning from near-misses, control failures, and industry incidents. By analyzing trends and lessons learned, we can identify emerging risks and improve controls before incidents occur.

**In practice:**
- Data analysis identifies trends and patterns
- Lessons learned are captured and shared
- Benchmarking against industry data reveals opportunities
- Predictive insights highlight rising risk potential
- Continuous improvement is embedded in the process

## Relationship to Five Pillars Methodology

These operational pillars complement the [Five Pillars of Best Practice Critical Risk Management](./five-pillars-of-best-practice-critical-risk-management.md):

- **Five Pillars Methodology:** Describes the process framework (Identify, Implement, Verify, Monitor, Learn)
- **CRM Operational Pillars:** Describes the principles that guide how we operate within that framework

Together, they provide both the structure (methodology) and the principles (operational pillars) needed for effective CRM implementation.

## Application in Forwood One

Forwood One is designed to support all five operational pillars:

- **Focus on Fatal Risks:** Platform helps identify and prioritize only SIF-potential risks
- **Define Critical Controls:** Tools for documenting and managing critical controls
- **Verify in the Field:** Mobile-first verification with frontline engagement
- **Ensure Data Transparency:** Real-time dashboards and reporting at all levels
- **Learn and Prevent:** Analytics, benchmarking, and predictive insights
