## Understanding Critical Risks

In the workplace, critical risks are hazards capable of causing serious, permanent harm or death.

Each type of work or operation presents different critical risks, meaning it's essential to:

- Identify these risks in context
- Target and prioritize interventions for each specific environment

---

## Hazards vs Risks

Two related but distinct concepts are often confused: **Hazards** and **Risks**.

### Hazard

Any potential source of damage, harm, or adverse effect to a person or asset.

**Example:** A busy road is a hazard because it poses a danger of being struck by a vehicle.

### Risk

The probability that a person will experience harm or health effects when exposed to a hazard.

The level of risk can vary depending on conditions:

- Crossing a highway poses a higher risk than crossing a quiet back road
- Staying inside a house removes exposure to the hazard, reducing the risk to zero

**Key Distinction:** While hazards describe what could cause harm, risks describe how likely it is that harm will occur.

## The Role of Control Measures

Organizations implement various preventive measures to control risk exposure. However, these measures are never 100% effective—gaps can occur through:

- Oversights
- Lack of diligence
- Failures in maintenance or procedure

When an accident occurs, it means all preventive measures failed. By analyzing the chain of events, one can identify which barriers failed and which, if correctly applied, might have prevented the incident.

### The Swiss Cheese Model

This principle is illustrated by the Reason (Swiss Cheese) Model, where multiple layers of defense (like slices of cheese) each have holes—and accidents occur when those holes align.

## The 14 Critical Risks

Critical Risk Management focuses on 14 categories of high-potential hazards:

1. Confined spaces
2. Entanglement or collapse
3. Unplanned explosive initiation
4. Falling from height
5. Vehicle and pedestrian collision
6. Slope failure
7. Vehicle collision and rollover
8. Electrical hazards
9. Hoisting operations
10. Exposure to hazardous substances
11. Drowning
12. Falling objects
13. Uncontrolled energy release
14. Fire and dangerous atmosphere (including underground environments)

Each of these requires tailored control measures suited to its specific risk profile.

## Five-Step Critical Risk Management Process

Effective critical risk management follows a structured five-step approach:

### 1. Planning

Define the tasks to be performed and assign clear responsibilities.

### 2. Identification

Inventory and identify all critical risks present in the targeted workplace or operation.

### 3. Estimation

Evaluate the level of each identified risk.

### 4. Implementation

Apply suitable control measures to reduce risk exposure to acceptable levels.

### 5. Follow-up

Monitor and verify that controls remain effective and appropriate over time.
