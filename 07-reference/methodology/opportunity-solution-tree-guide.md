# Opportunity Solution Tree (OST) Guide

A guide to using the Opportunity Solution Tree framework for product strategy.

## Overview

The Opportunity Solution Tree (OST) is a framework developed by Teresa Torres that helps product teams systematically explore and address customer needs to achieve desired outcomes. It structures work hierarchically:

**Business Outcomes** → **Product Outcomes** → **Opportunities** → **Solutions** → **Experiments**

## Framework Hierarchy

### 1. Business Outcomes

High-level business goals that the organization wants to achieve. Examples:
- Increase revenue by X%
- Reduce customer churn by Y%
- Expand into new market segments

**Characteristics:**
- Measurable
- Business-focused
- Strategic in nature
- Typically owned by executive leadership

### 2. Product Outcomes

Product-specific outcomes that support business outcomes. Examples:
- Increase user activation rate
- Improve feature adoption
- Reduce time to value

**Characteristics:**
- Measurable
- Product-focused
- Directly influence business outcomes
- Owned by product teams

### 3. Opportunities

Customer needs, pain points, or desires that, if addressed, will drive the desired outcomes. Examples:
- Users struggle to find key features
- Onboarding process is too complex
- Users need better ways to collaborate

**Characteristics:**
- Customer-focused (not solution-focused)
- Linked to specific outcomes
- Based on research and evidence
- Multiple opportunities can support one outcome

### 4. Solutions

Potential ways to address identified opportunities. Examples:
- Redesign navigation structure
- Create interactive onboarding tutorial
- Add collaboration features

**Characteristics:**
- Solution-focused
- Linked to specific opportunities
- Multiple solutions can address one opportunity
- Should be validated before full implementation

### 5. Experiments

Tests designed to validate or invalidate proposed solutions. Examples:
- A/B test new navigation
- Usability test onboarding flow
- Prototype collaboration features

**Characteristics:**
- Hypothesis-driven
- Linked to specific solutions
- Results inform go/no-go decisions
- Multiple experiments can test one solution

## Best Practices

### Starting with Outcomes

1. **Define Business Outcomes First**
   - Work with leadership to identify key business outcomes
   - Ensure outcomes are measurable and time-bound
   - Document in `04-opportunities/01-business-outcomes/`

2. **Identify Product Outcomes**
   - Determine which product outcomes support each business outcome
   - Ensure product outcomes are measurable
   - Document in `04-opportunities/02-product-outcomes/`

### Discovering Opportunities

1. **Use Research**
   - Conduct user research to identify customer needs
   - Analyze data to find pain points
   - Synthesize insights into opportunities
   - Document research in `03-discovery/research/`

2. **Focus on Customer Needs**
   - Opportunities should describe customer needs, not solutions
   - Avoid solution language in opportunity descriptions
   - Use customer language and perspective

3. **Link to Outcomes**
   - Each opportunity should link to a product outcome
   - Multiple opportunities can support one outcome
   - Document opportunities in `04-opportunities/03-opportunities/`

### Exploring Solutions

1. **Generate Multiple Solutions**
   - Don't jump to the first solution
   - Brainstorm multiple ways to address each opportunity
   - Consider different approaches and trade-offs

2. **Link to Opportunities**
   - Each solution should clearly address a specific opportunity
   - Multiple solutions can address one opportunity
   - Document solutions in `04-opportunities/04-solutions/`

### Validating with Experiments

1. **Design Experiments**
   - Create hypotheses for each solution
   - Design experiments to test hypotheses
   - Define success criteria

2. **Run Experiments**
   - Execute experiments systematically
   - Collect data and observations
   - Document in `04-opportunities/05-experiments/`

3. **Make Decisions**
   - Analyze experiment results
   - Decide whether to proceed, iterate, or abandon
   - Update solution status based on results

## Linking Strategy

Maintain clear links between levels:
- Business outcomes link to product outcomes
- Product outcomes link to opportunities
- Opportunities link to solutions
- Solutions link to experiments
- All levels can link to research that informs them

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Skipping Levels**: Don't jump from outcomes directly to solutions
2. **Solution-Focused Opportunities**: Opportunities should describe customer needs, not solutions
3. **Weak Links**: Ensure clear connections between levels
4. **Ignoring Experiments**: Always validate solutions before full implementation
5. **Not Updating**: Keep documents current as work progresses
