## What Causes Workplace Fatalities?

**Workplace fatalities occur when critical risks aren't properly identified or effectively controlled.**

Critical risks in safety are risks with the potential to cause serious injury or death. Poorly managed critical risk significantly increases the consequences of incidents—and the likelihood of a very serious or potentially fatal outcome.

### Key Challenges of Managing Critical Risks

Traditional safety approaches overly rely on injury statistics rather than proactively identifying and controlling risks. From recent research, we know that managing minor injuries compared to managing serious injuries or fatalities requires a very different approach.

**Case Study:** A recent study highlights how the construction industry continues to experience high fatality rates due to an over-reliance on lagging indicators and ineffective control implementation. When a Critical Risk Management (CRM) program was introduced to proactively identify and verify critical controls, the research found:

- **Hazard reporting improved by 8%**
- **High-potential incidents reduced by 80%**

_Reference: Selleck, R. J. (2023). A critical control approach to preventing fatalities in construction. Edith Cowan University._

## The #1 SIF Prevention Strategy

CRM simplifies and streamlines the management of critical risk by installing a clear process for ensuring risks are effectively controlled before work commences.

### Critical Risk Management: Simple and Effective

Clear, simple, and practical, the process integrates within existing procedures to embed safety firmly within operations. This approach shifts safety from a compliance-driven obligation to an ingrained, proactive work practice, ensuring that fatal risk awareness and mitigation become second nature to every worker.

**Critical Risk Management is designed to prevent serious accidents by empowering workers to make better, risk-informed decisions.** By providing clarity, visibility, and confidence, CRM ensures everyone knows and plays their part in creating a fatality-free workplace.

#### The Three Fundamental Principles

1. **Identify and understand critical risks**
2. **Implement and maintain effective critical controls**
3. **Rigorously verify and monitor control effectiveness**

## Strategy #1: Know Your Critical Risks

### Not All Risks Are Equal

CRM prioritizes the hazards most likely to cause fatalities or serious injuries. By laser-focusing on these critical risks, we can significantly improve safety outcomes.

Many common safety programs focus on reducing minor hazards, removing near-misses, and modifying unsafe behaviors in the hope this will lead to a reduction in serious incidents and fatalities. This approach not only consumes considerable resources—it comes at a significant cost. **Billions of dollars are invested in these initiatives, yet workplace fatalities continue to occur.**

### Target the Fatality Zone

Instead of spreading resources thinly, CRM targets the **Fatality Zone**—the small subset of events, also called "high-potential incidents," with a credible risk of fatality.

By targeting events that are truly life-threatening, we can:
- Identify critical risks
- Implement effective critical controls
- Efficiently allocate resources to directly prevent serious injury or death

### Critical Risk Example: Fall from Height

Tragically, around **57,000 construction and utilities workers lose their lives from falls every year.** Over the past 5 years, most fatalities in these industries were caused by falls from:
- Buildings and other structures
- Ladders
- Trucks, semi-trailers, and lorries
- Openings in floors, walls, or ceilings
- Scaffolding
- Steps and stairways
- Power hoists

**We know how to work safely at heights, so why do these tragedies keep happening?**

**Because this critical risk is not being adequately controlled.**

## Strategy #2: Implement Effective Critical Controls

### The Right Way to Manage Critical Risks

In Critical Risk Management, a small set of critical risks are identified and for each critical risk a manageable number of critical controls are implemented to reduce the impacts of a potential serious injury or fatality event.

**Critical controls aren't just general safety measures—they're essential to protect workers from serious injury or fatality.**

### Requirements for Effective Critical Controls

As a general guide, critical controls must be:
- **Purpose-built** — Designed specifically for the critical risk
- **Independent** — Not reliant on other controls
- **Reliable** — Proven to work consistently
- **Repeatable** — Able to be implemented the same way each time
- **Measurable** — Observable and auditable

### Example: Fall Protection Controls

The critical controls to prevent fatal falls could include:
- Fall protection equipment
- Open edge protection

**Before commencing work at height, all relevant critical controls must be verified as in place and operational to be considered effective.**

## Strategy #3: Verify and Monitor Critical Controls

### Controls on Their Own Are Not Enough

A robust verification process is vital to guarantee that critical controls are functioning as intended when high-risk work tasks are performed.

### Multiple Control Failures Lead to Fatalities

Workplace fatalities are often due to **multiple control failures**. For the critical risk Fall from Height, such failures include:

**Design Flaws**
- Missing anchor points
- Inadequate structural considerations

**Organizational Issues**
- Inadequate training
- Unclear procedures
- Poor communication

**Unsafe Working Conditions**
- Fear of stopping work due to production/time pressures
- Inadequate resources
- Environmental factors

When failures such as these align, the likelihood of a fatality significantly increases.

### The Verification Process

**Critical Risk Management reduces the risk by systematically verifying critical controls and correcting any failures before an incident can occur.**

In the Critical Risk Management process, critical controls must be **physically verified as in place and operational before any work commences.** Verifications are completed via simple operator, supervisor, and manager checklists in the Forwood CRM+ solution.

### Layered Defense Strategy

**Multiple verification layers are known to reduce human error and strengthen reliability of safety checks.** If one layer of defense weakens, another layer steps in, ensuring fatal risks do not escalate into incidents.

### Supervisors and Managers Transform Safety

At the same time, the participation of supervisors and managers in the verification process transforms safety into a visible, operational priority for all personnel.

**If a critical control is missing or not working, work must STOP until it is fixed.** Workers must know they have the authority to stop work if a critical control fails—it keeps themselves and their team safe.

## Strategy #4: Sustain Your CRM Program

### Leverage Data for Continuous Improvement

Overall, maintaining a successful CRM program requires disciplined verification, strong leadership accountability, and consistent frontline engagement.

**One key challenge is maintaining critical control effectiveness over time.** This can be overcome by adopting a number of data-driven strategies from the outset, including:

- **Identify who in the organization will be accountable for each critical risk**
- **Embed Critical Risk Management in your company Objectives and Key Results**
- **Operationalize critical control ownership and link assurance to in-field verification**
- **Monitor critical controls using a comprehensive digital solution**
- **Perform regular audits to ensure ongoing critical controls effectiveness**
- **Measure success with leading and lagging safety metrics aligned to your maturity**
- **Benchmark performance against other organizations in your industry**

### Building a Safety Culture

**At the end of the day, effective CRM isn't just about following a process—it's about creating a culture where both protecting lives and your company assets are the highest priority.**

Forwood's Critical Risk Management community fosters benchmarking and learning together. By integrating digital assurance, real-time verification, and leadership engagement, our solution empowers businesses to operationalize risk ownership, strengthen their safety maturity, and drive measurable improvements in fatality prevention.
