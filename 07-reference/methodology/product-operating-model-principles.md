# Product Operating Model Principles

Marty Cagan's Product Model First Principles - governing logic for AI-assisted product planning and empowered product teams.

## Purpose

These principles form the **governing logic** that the AI agent should use when creating, refining, or prioritizing product specifications and plans.

They define how **empowered product teams** think, decide, and deliver within modern product operating models.

**Each principle represents a constraint or bias to apply during ideation, planning, and documentation.**

---

## How the AI Should Apply These Principles

When producing a specification, the AI should:

- **Apply all relevant principles contextually** across the planning flow (strategy → discovery → delivery → culture)
- **Justify feature choices** using the principles as reasoning anchors
- **Detect conflicts** between principles (e.g., predictability vs. innovation) and document trade-offs transparently
- **Propose alignment actions** where organizational or technical constraints block adherence

---

## 1. Product Team Principles

_Define how empowered product teams operate_

### Empowered with Problems to Solve

The AI should frame specifications as **problem statements**, not feature orders. Each plan must clearly define the user or business problem the team owns, rather than prescribing fixed outputs.

### Outcomes over Output

Each planned initiative must **link to measurable outcomes** — such as adoption, engagement, or conversion — not feature counts or deadlines.

### Sense of Ownership

The AI should **assign ownership boundaries clearly**. Each feature area should have a single accountable team or role with autonomy over discovery and delivery decisions.

### Collaboration

The AI should assume that **effective execution depends on continuous collaboration** between PM, design, and engineering — discovery and delivery are joint, not sequential.

---

## 2. Product Strategy Principles

_Guide high-level planning and prioritization_

### Focus

The AI should **limit planned initiatives to a few** that directly drive core objectives. Avoid parallel or low-impact efforts.

### Powered by Insights

Strategy sections **must reference validated insights** — user data, market trends, customer research — not assumptions.

### Transparency

The **rationale behind prioritization, trade-offs, and risk decisions** must be explicit and explainable.

### Placing Bets

Encourage **bold yet evidence-backed initiatives**. The AI should identify calculated risks that could deliver step-change outcomes, rather than incremental changes only.

---

## 3. Product Discovery Principles

_Shape how the AI handles uncertainty and idea validation_

### Minimize Waste

Plans should **emphasize validation early** to prevent unnecessary development. The AI should propose prototypes, experiments, or tests before full implementation.

### Assess Product Risks

For each planned feature, the AI **must explicitly assess** value, usability, feasibility, and viability risks (Marty Cagan's four-risk framework).

### Embrace Rapid Experimentation

When uncertainty is high, the AI should **propose iterative tests**, not one-shot builds.

### Test Ideas Responsibly

The AI **must ensure ethical, compliant, and user-respectful testing practices** (e.g., informed consent, no dark patterns).

---

## 4. Product Delivery Principles

_Define how solutions should be built and released_

### Small, Frequent, Uncoupled Releases

The AI should **structure delivery into small, testable increments** that can be released independently. Avoid large, coupled projects.

### Instrumentation

**All features must include instrumentation** for measuring success — e.g., telemetry, analytics events, feedback hooks.

### Monitoring

Specifications should **include post-release monitoring plans** to ensure product health, performance, and user outcomes are tracked.

### Deployment Infrastructure

The AI should ensure that **technical planning assumes modern CI/CD practices** and scalable deployment pipelines.

---

## 5. Product Culture Principles

_Ensure the resulting system reinforces the right behaviours_

### Principles over Process

The AI should **follow guiding values** — empowerment, learning, and impact — over rigid templates or bureaucratic steps.

### Trust over Control

Specifications should **delegate authority to teams**, avoiding unnecessary approval gates or micro-management.

### Innovation over Predictability

The AI should **prioritize adaptability and exploration**, acknowledging that innovation often introduces uncertainty.

### Learning over Failure

**Encourage iteration and reflection**. Plans should include mechanisms to capture and act on lessons learned from both success and failure.

---

## How the AI Should Use These 20 Principles

When generating specifications or roadmaps:

1. **Ground every decision** in one or more of the 20 principles
2. **Flag misalignments** (e.g., if a plan favors control over trust)
3. **Integrate measurable outcomes and learning loops** into every proposal
4. **Balance strategy and discovery** — ensure ideas are both visionary and validated
5. **Recommend organizational enablers** (e.g., instrumentation, feedback loops, trust-based governance)

## Application in Product Development

### During Planning

- **Apply Product Team Principles:** Frame as problems, focus on outcomes, assign ownership, enable collaboration
- **Apply Strategy Principles:** Focus on few initiatives, power by insights, be transparent, place calculated bets
- **Apply Discovery Principles:** Minimize waste, assess risks, experiment rapidly, test responsibly
- **Apply Delivery Principles:** Small releases, instrument everything, monitor outcomes, use modern infrastructure
- **Apply Culture Principles:** Principles over process, trust over control, innovation over predictability, learning over failure

### During Specification

- Ground every decision in one or more principles
- Flag misalignments with principles
- Integrate measurable outcomes and learning loops
- Balance strategy and discovery
- Recommend organizational enablers
