# Product Discovery Process

A guide to conducting effective product discovery.

## Overview

Product discovery is the process of learning about customers, their needs, and potential solutions before building. This guide outlines best practices for discovery work in this knowledge base.

## Discovery Activities

### 1. Research

Research activities help us understand customers, markets, and data:

#### User Research
- **Interviews**: One-on-one conversations with users
- **Surveys**: Quantitative data collection from users
- **Usability Studies**: Observing users interact with products
- **Field Studies**: Observing users in their natural environment

**Location:** `03-discovery/research/user-research/`

#### Market Research
- **Competitive Analysis**: Understanding competitors and alternatives
- **Industry Trends**: Tracking market and industry developments
- **Market Segmentation**: Understanding different customer segments

**Location:** `03-discovery/research/market-research/`

#### Data Analysis
- **Analytics**: Quantitative analysis of product usage
- **Metrics Analysis**: Understanding key performance indicators
- **Cohort Analysis**: Analyzing user behavior by cohort

**Location:** `03-discovery/research/data-analysis/`

### 2. Synthesis

Transform research into actionable insights:

#### Insights
- Synthesize findings from multiple research activities
- Identify patterns and themes
- Document key learnings

**Location:** `03-discovery/insights/`

#### Opportunities
- Translate insights into opportunities
- Link opportunities to outcomes
- Prioritize opportunities

**Location:** `04-opportunities/03-opportunities/`

### 3. Ideation

Generate potential solutions:

#### Solution Brainstorming
- Generate multiple solutions for each opportunity
- Consider different approaches
- Evaluate trade-offs

**Location:** `04-opportunities/04-solutions/`

### 4. Validation

Test solutions before building:

#### Experiments
- Design experiments to test solutions
- Run experiments systematically
- Analyze results

**Location:** `04-opportunities/05-experiments/`

## Discovery Workflow

### 1. Start with Questions

What do we need to learn? What assumptions need validation?

### 2. Choose Research Methods

Select appropriate research methods based on:
- What you need to learn
- Available resources
- Time constraints
- Access to users/data

### 3. Conduct Research

Execute research activities:
- Schedule interviews
- Deploy surveys
- Analyze data
- Document findings

### 4. Synthesize Findings

- Review all research
- Identify patterns
- Create insights
- Document in `03-discovery/insights/`

### 5. Generate Opportunities

- Translate insights into opportunities
- Link to outcomes
- Prioritize
- Document in `04-opportunities/03-opportunities/`

### 6. Explore Solutions

- Brainstorm solutions
- Evaluate options
- Document in `04-opportunities/04-solutions/`

### 7. Validate Solutions

- Design experiments
- Run tests
- Analyze results
- Make decisions
- Document in `04-opportunities/05-experiments/`

## Best Practices

### Research

1. **Start with Questions**: Know what you're trying to learn
2. **Use Multiple Methods**: Combine qualitative and quantitative research
3. **Talk to Real Users**: Get out of the building
4. **Document Everything**: Record findings for future reference
5. **Share Learnings**: Make research accessible to the team

### Synthesis

1. **Look for Patterns**: Identify themes across research
2. **Stay Customer-Focused**: Keep customer needs at the center
3. **Link to Outcomes**: Connect insights to business and product outcomes
4. **Prioritize**: Focus on highest-impact opportunities

### Validation

1. **Test Early**: Validate before building
2. **Start Small**: Use low-fidelity experiments when possible
3. **Measure What Matters**: Focus on outcome metrics
4. **Learn and Iterate**: Use results to inform next steps

## Linking Discovery to Strategy

Discovery work should always link to:
- **Business Outcomes**: How does this support business goals?
- **Product Outcomes**: How does this support product goals?
- **Opportunities**: What opportunities does this inform?
- **Solutions**: What solutions does this validate?

## Continuous Discovery

Discovery is not a one-time activity:
- Conduct research regularly
- Update insights as you learn
- Revisit opportunities based on new information
- Validate solutions continuously
