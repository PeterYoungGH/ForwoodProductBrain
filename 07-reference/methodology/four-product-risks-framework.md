# Four Product Risks Framework

Marty Cagan's framework for evaluating whether an idea is truly viable before investing heavily in design and development. Emphasizes early, evidence-based validation across four fundamental risk areas.

## Purpose

Marty Cagan's **Four Risks Framework** helps product teams evaluate whether an idea is truly viable before investing heavily in design and development. It emphasizes **early, evidence-based validation** across four fundamental risk areas — Value, Usability, Feasibility, and Business Viability.

It serves as both a **discovery compass** and a **planning filter**: teams should address each risk explicitly during planning and specification to ensure every feature or product concept is desirable, usable, buildable, and sustainable.

---

## The Four Risks

### 1. Value Risk

**Question:** Will customers buy this (or choose to use it)?

**Focus:** Whether the product or feature creates real value for the target user or customer segment.

**Key Considerations:**

- Does it solve a genuine, high-priority problem?
- Does it outperform current alternatives?
- Will customers pay (with money, time, or attention)?
- Have hypotheses been validated with data or user feedback?

**Desired Outcome:**  
The product is **desirable** — users clearly see the value proposition and are motivated to engage.

---

### 2. Usability Risk

**Question:** Can users figure out how to use it?

**Focus:** The ease with which users can understand and achieve their goals.

**Key Considerations:**

- Can target users intuitively complete key tasks?
- Does the interface align with their mental models and expectations?
- Have you tested early prototypes with real users?
- Are usability issues identified and resolved before delivery?

**Desired Outcome:**  
The product is **intuitive and pleasant to use**, minimizing friction or confusion.

---

### 3. Feasibility Risk

**Question:** Can we build it with the time, skills, and technology available?

**Focus:** The technical and operational ability of the team to deliver the proposed solution.

**Key Considerations:**

- Are the technical assumptions sound?
- Do we have the necessary infrastructure, data, and expertise?
- Are dependencies, scalability, and performance constraints known?
- Has engineering been engaged early to identify constraints or trade-offs?

**Desired Outcome:**  
The product **can be built efficiently and reliably** with existing resources or planned investment.

---

### 4. Business Viability Risk

**Question:** Can the solution work for our business?

**Focus:** Ensuring alignment with the organization's goals, model, and constraints.

**Key Considerations:**

- Is the idea financially sustainable?
- Does it comply with legal, privacy, and policy constraints?
- Is it aligned with brand, operations, and strategy?
- Will stakeholders (e.g., sales, marketing, finance) support it?

**Desired Outcome:**  
The product **supports business objectives** and can scale without breaking organizational constraints.

---

## Application in Planning and Specification

During feature planning, the AI agent or product team should:

1. **Frame each feature as a set of hypotheses** across all four risks
2. **Identify evidence** (user research, prototype results, tech validation, or stakeholder alignment) that reduces each risk
3. **Highlight unknowns** requiring discovery or testing before full specification
4. **Use the framework to decide** which features move forward, need more discovery, or should be re-scoped

**This ensures specifications are grounded in validated insight rather than assumptions.**

## Integration with Prototyping Workflow

The Four Risks Framework complements other product frameworks by:

- **Validating ideas early** before investing in design and development
- **Reducing waste** by identifying non-viable concepts before specification
- **Providing structure** for discovery and validation activities
- **Enabling confident prioritization** based on evidence across all four dimensions

Teams should apply this framework alongside the **Product Delight Checklist** to ensure features are both fundamentally viable and emotionally resonant.
