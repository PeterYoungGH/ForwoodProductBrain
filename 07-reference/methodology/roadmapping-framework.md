# Roadmapping Framework

**Last Updated:** 2025-12-16  
**Source:** [Forwood Roadmapping Framework](../../00-inbox/raw/2025-12-09-forwood-roadmapping-framework-[TO-DO].md)

## Overview

This document outlines Forwood's roadmapping framework, describing how we structure roadmaps across multiple horizons, update frequencies, rituals, and decision criteria. The framework provides a clear, consistent operating model that enables Forwood to deliver quickly while maintaining long-term strategic alignment.

## Long-Term Vision & Strategy (2+ Year Horizon)

Forwood's roadmapping framework is anchored beneath a longer-term **two-year (or multi-year) vision and strategy**. This top-level vision provides direction, guardrails, and a sense of purpose, while still allowing for evolution as the market and product mature.

**Purpose of the 2+ Year Vision:**
- Establish fundamental key outcomes and the destinations the company is moving toward
- Define the long-term value the organisation aims to create
- Identify the foundational capabilities needed across the product ecosystem
- Provide stability so shorter-horizon roadmaps don't drift or conflict

**How It Connects to the Roadmapping Framework:**
- The **Strategic Roadmap** (6–12 months) translates the 2+ year vision into actionable themes, bets, and areas of focused investment
- The **Portfolio Roadmap** then breaks those themes into initiatives
- The **Delivery Roadmap** executes on those initiatives through frequent releases
- The **Public Roadmap** selectively communicates elements of the journey that align with the long-term narrative

This ensures that:
- Daily and weekly releases contribute to meaningful progress toward the vision
- Launch moments reinforce the strategic narrative
- Teams have clarity without excessive upfront specification

## Roadmap Layers Overview

Forwood uses four roadmap layers, each serving a different audience and purpose:

1. **Strategic Roadmap** – Themes and bets, 6–12 month horizon
2. **Portfolio / Product-Area Roadmap** – Initiatives and outcomes, 3–6 month horizon
3. **Delivery Roadmap** – Release-level execution (weekly/ongoing)
4. **Public Roadmap** – External communication without commitments

## Update Frequencies by Roadmap Layer

### Strategic Roadmap (Exec Level)

- **Responsible:** Chief Product Officer (CPO), Senior Product Manager, Product Marketing Manager
- **Update Frequency:** Quarterly (lightly), plus mid-quarter check-ins
- **Why:** Strategic priorities shift slowly; updates follow major learning or changes in direction

### Portfolio / Product-Area Roadmap (Product + Eng Leaders)

- **Responsible:** Senior Product Manager in consultation with Engineering Leads
- **Update Frequency:** Fortnightly or monthly
- **Why:** Initiative sequencing changes as teams learn, validate, or uncover constraints

### Delivery Roadmap (Squad Level)

- **Responsible:** Engineering Manager or Delivery Manager
- **Update Frequency:** Weekly or as releases are prepared
- **Why:** Frequent, small releases require continuous refinement of scope, sequencing, and readiness
- **Focus:** Delivery roadmaps focus on **release planning**, not sprints, and aim to support a rapid, continuous delivery model

### Public Roadmap (Marketing + Product)

- **Responsible:** Senior Product Manager, Chief Product Officer, Product Marketing Manager
- **Update Frequency:** Monthly or quarterly
- **Why:** Must minimise churn and avoid over-communication of changes

## Rituals for Updating Roadmaps

### Strategic Roadmap Rituals

- Quarterly strategy review with execs
- Review market shifts, customer insights, major bets, learnings
- Reconfirm themes; cut or reshape anything losing strategic relevance

### Portfolio Roadmap Rituals

- Monthly initiative review with product, engineering, and design leads
- Evaluate initiative progress and new insights
- Reassign priorities based on:
  - Discovery learnings
  - Capacity changes
  - Cross-team dependencies
  - Emerging opportunities
- Update "Now / Next / Later" sections

### Delivery Roadmap Rituals

- Weekly release readiness check ("What ships next?")
- Continuous backlog refinement aligned to release opportunities
- Release packaging sessions to determine what is included in the next deployable increment
- Technical risk review for upcoming releases
- Regular demos to close the loop on completed increments
- Emphasis on **shipping small, frequent releases**, with occasional bundled **launch moments**

### Public Roadmap Rituals

- Monthly check with product + marketing
- Confirm what can safely be shared publicly
- Retire or reframe items that are no longer planned

## Criteria for Deciding What Moves In, Out, or Across Horizons

### Strategic Roadmap Criteria

- Aligns with company objectives
- Supports core strategic bets
- Has the potential to materially shift metrics (activation, retention, ARR)
- Not overly dependent on unproven technology or partners
- Large enough to matter, but small enough to execute through initiative layers

### Portfolio Roadmap Criteria

- Clear opportunity backed by user evidence or data
- Feasible within current or planned capacity
- Strong alignment to principles and product direction
- Delivers measurable outcome improvements
- Dependencies are known and manageable
- Provides optionality (i.e., not a single-solution lock-in)

### Delivery Roadmap Criteria

- Ready for execution (shaped enough that engineering can proceed)
- Fits team capacity for the upcoming sprint
- Unblocked and technically feasible
- Supports the outcomes defined in the portfolio roadmap
- Has validated assumptions or a limited-risk experiment planned

### Public Roadmap Criteria

- No regulatory or commercial risk in sharing
- Direction is stable enough not to cause confusion or misalignment
- Meaningfully improves customer understanding of where the product is going

## Example Flow: How a Feature Moves Through the System

1. **Customer signal** arrives (qualitative + quantitative)
2. Added to opportunity backlog
3. Reviewed in portfolio monthly session
4. If promising, shaped into a problem statement and outcome definition
5. Placed in **Next** in the portfolio roadmap
6. When resourced, moved to **Now**
7. Passed to delivery for sprint breakdown
8. Added to the public roadmap only once direction is firm

## Principles That Keep Roadmaps Fast and Flexible

- Prioritise **problems**, not features
- Use **bets**, not commitments
- Keep **long-range detail fuzzy**
- Update **near-term plans frequently**, long-term plans lightly
- Anchor everything in **clear outcomes and metrics**
- Maintain **strong product principles** so teams do not rely on detailed instructions

## Roadmap Templates

### Strategic Roadmap Template (6–12 Months)

**Purpose:** Communicate themes, bets, and strategic intent without committing to specific features.

**Typical Structure:**
- **Vision Statement** (1–2 sentences)
- **Strategic Themes / Pillars** (3–5 max)
- **Bets / Opportunity Areas**
  - Problem / Opportunity
  - Desired Outcome (metric shift)
  - Why Now (strategic rationale)
- **High-Level Timeline:** Now / Next / Later (no dates)
- **Success Measures:** Activation, Retention, ARR, Efficiency, Market Expansion

**Graphics Often Used:**
- Theme-based swimlane diagram
- Strategy pyramid (Vision → Themes → Bets)
- Now / Next / Later blocks with colour-coded priority

### Portfolio / Product-Area Roadmap Template (3–6 Months)

**Purpose:** Show how initiatives ladder up to strategy, allocate team focus, and define **major launch moments**.

**Typical Structure:**
- **Product Area / Domain**
- **Initiatives (Problem-Based):**
  - Problem Statement
  - Opportunity Summary
  - Expected Outcome
  - Key Metrics
- **Dependencies & Constraints**
- **Now / Next / Later or Quarterly Buckets**
- **Initiative Status:** Exploring / Validating / Building / Measuring
- **Launch Moments:**
  - Features or initiative sets bundled into a coherent market-facing launch
  - Launch timing guidance (month or quarter, not date-specific)
- **Resourcing Overview:** Team allocation at initiative level

**Graphics Often Used:**
- Gantt-lite bar visualisation (wide time blocks, not day-level precision)
- Kanban-style Now / Next / Later board
- Initiative heatmap (value vs. effort, outcome vs. confidence)
- Dedicated "Launch Moments" call-outs or banners

### Delivery Roadmap Template (Release-Oriented Execution)

**Purpose:** Move work into production through rapid, frequent releases while maintaining clarity on upcoming deployable increments.

**Typical Structure:**
- **Release Goals** (what value is intended in the next release)
- **Release Candidate Items** (features, enhancements, fixes)
- **Readiness Status:** Ready / In Progress / Needs Clarification / Blocked
- **Risks & Assumptions**
- **Technical Improvements / Platform Work**
- **Target Release Window:** ASAP, This Week, This Month (not date-locked)
- **Launch Alignment:** Whether this release contributes to an upcoming launch event

**Graphics Often Used:**
- Release pipeline board (Ready → In Progress → In QA → Ready to Release → Released)
- Release milestone line diagram
- Cumulative flow diagram for release throughput (optional)

### Public Roadmap Template (External-Friendly)

**Purpose:** Inform customers about investment areas without overcommitting.

**Typical Structure:**
- **Themes (User Outcomes Framed):** "Improving Collaboration", "Increasing Speed", "Enhancing Mobile Experience"
- **Items Categorised Into:**
  - **Planned**
  - **In Progress**
  - **Recently Released**
- **Each Item Includes:**
  - Short description (problem-focused)
  - Expected user benefit
  - Status label

**Graphics Often Used:**
- Three-column layout (Planned / In Progress / Released)
- Clean iconography to indicate categories
- Limited/no timelines

## Supporting Artefacts

### Initiative Brief Template

- Problem Statement
- Evidence / Insights
- Outcome & Success Measures
- Users Impacted
- Constraints & Dependencies
- Options Explored
- Experiment Plan / Milestones

### Opportunity Backlog Template

- Opportunity Name
- Problem Summary
- Signals (Data, Customer Feedback)
- Confidence Level
- Impact Estimate
- Effort Estimate (Rough)
- Strategic Alignment Score
