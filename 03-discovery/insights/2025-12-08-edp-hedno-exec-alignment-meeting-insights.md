## Insight Statement

EDP and HEDNO executive meetings require an empathy-first messaging framework that shifts from past promises to demonstrable improvements, emphasizes stability and configurability, and uses Macquarie portfolio alignment as a supporting narrative. Both clients have acute sensitivities (EDP's recent fatality and performance perception, HEDNO's stability concerns) that require tailored approaches while maintaining standardisation objectives.

---

## Evidence

### Messaging Framework Principles

**Core Messaging Approach:**
- Lead with empathy and acknowledge frustration, especially given EDP's recent fatality and Hedno's instability issues
- Shift away from past promises and "sizzle without delivery" by grounding conversation in practical, demonstrable examples
- Position Forwood One as a meaningful reset: stability, speed, configurability, and alignment to fatality prevention
- Anchor messaging in what matters to the client: what's in it for them
- Avoid statements that imply we will build bespoke solutions or deliver every request; avoid promising future dates prematurely

**Implication:** Messaging must be empathetic, practical, and focused on demonstrable value rather than promises.

### Handling Outstanding Requests

**Request Management Strategy:**
- Acknowledge all requests; do not commit to specifics in these meetings
- Explain the structured January roadmap session where all feature requests will be reviewed against standardisation, best-practice fatality prevention, platform stability, and configuration over customisation
- Reposition the narrative so requests are evaluated through a wider lens: multi-client impact, industry standardisation, Macquarie alignment, and safety outcomes
- Clarify that many requests conflict across clients (e.g., EDP vs Hedno contractor workflows), and part of our role is to rationalise and standardise

**Implication:** Feature requests must be evaluated through a standardisation lens, not as individual client commitments.

### Strategic Positioning - Macquarie Portfolio Angle

**Portfolio Narrative:**
- Both EDP and Hedno operate under the Macquarie portfolio
- Suggested narrative: We have made undertakings to Macquarie to bring best-practice CRM methods from mining into utilities
- Standardisation across utilities is a key objective
- Forwood One is the platform designed to achieve that
- Careful wording required: avoid implying Macquarie issued directives; frame it as undertakings we have made based on conversations and shared objectives

**Implication:** Macquarie portfolio alignment is a strategic narrative that supports standardisation, but must be framed carefully to avoid implying directives.

### Sizzle Items - What to Show

**Safe to Show:**
- Configurability improvements (e.g., question editing, checklist configurability)
- A thin slice of the new UI demonstrating simplicity and stability
- AI-related early features that are real and safe to show (AI risk ID example, weekly insights email) – only if fully stable
- Demonstrable components only; no speculative roadmap visuals

**Items to Avoid:**
- V2 features not yet in build
- Contractor company hierarchy feature (not ready)
- Any area where timelines are not yet reliable
- Anything requiring cross-module demonstration before platform integration is complete

**Implication:** Demos must focus on stable, demonstrable features only - no speculation or uncommitted timelines.

### Timeline Messaging

**Agreed Timeline Framework:**
- EDP Technical Release: 22 February 2026
- UAT: approximately 3 weeks post-release
- Deployment window for EDP: mid-March to early April (buffer required for UAT findings)
- Hedno migration: mid-2026 is the safest headline, but messaging needs flexibility
- Emphasise quality over speed, particularly due to Hedno's sensitivity around breakages
- Reinforce that Forwood One enables far more reliable, frequent, incremental releases

**Implication:** Timeline messaging must balance realism with flexibility, emphasizing quality over speed.

### EDP Meeting Strategy

**Key Risks for EDP:**
- High frustration, recent fatality, and perception of performance failure
- Belief that they already operate at very high safety maturity; avoid any maturity model framing that implies otherwise
- Historic over-promising has damaged credibility; avoid dates or specifics that aren't locked in

**Approach:**
- Use empathy first
- Demonstrate a small, real example of improvement
- Position the January roadmap meeting as the structured turning point
- Reinforce that configurability and stability are core improvements they will benefit from
- Use Macquarie alignment as a supporting narrative, not the lead pitch

**Implication:** EDP requires empathy-first approach with focus on demonstrable improvements, avoiding maturity model framing.

### Hedno Meeting Strategy

**Key Risks for Hedno:**
- Deep sensitivity to breakages in Safety+; unstable releases have caused their workforce to reject the tool
- Their original purchase driver was Geomap; not CRM
- Extremely hierarchical organisational culture; language and framing must be precise
- Heavy usage of DSS-built content rather than Forwood content; requires careful mapping

**Approach:**
- Focus on stability, reliability, and ease of use (particularly for field users)
- Reassure that release processes have been tightened: regression testing, QA governance, and gated deployments
- Begin shifting their mindset from bespoke requests to future configurability
- Acknowledge the legacy issues in Safety+ without undermining trust

**Implication:** Hedno requires focus on stability and reliability, with reassurance about improved release processes.
