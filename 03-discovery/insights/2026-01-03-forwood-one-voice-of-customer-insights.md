## Insight Statement

EHS professionals (our primary persona) are not trying to "improve safety metrics" or "roll out software" - they're trying to prevent catastrophic events in systems they don't fully control, and stop being the shock absorber between human failure and organisational denial. The market VoC reveals deep scepticism, defensiveness, and fatigue, while customer VoC shows clarity, alignment, and confidence - demonstrating the transformation Forwood enables. This contrast, along with detailed persona psychology, provides critical guidance for product development, messaging, and sales conversations.

---

## Evidence

### Primary Persona - The Real Problem They're Trying to Solve

**What we learned:** Safety System Owners are accountable for preventing fatalities but lack authority to control incentives, schedules, or production pressure. They're caught between leadership expectations, operational resistance, and personal ethics.

**Evidence:**
- "You're not doing enough, but please back off"
- "Almost got fired for enforcing LOTO"
- "I don't report to anyone in the building. I have a dotted line to the general manager and in some ways I see how disconnected they are"

**Why it matters:** They need tools that make fatal risk visible to those who DO have authority, not more administrative burden. The buying question is: "Will this finally help me do what I'm accountable for - or is it another system I have to manage that won't change outcomes?"

**Frequency:** Extremely high - appears across multiple threads and contexts.

### Primary Persona - Seven Core Pain Themes

**What we learned:** EHS professionals face seven interconnected pain themes that drive purchase decisions:

1. **Accountability Without Authority** - Responsible but powerless
2. **Drowning in Data, Starving for Action** - Overloaded with performative reporting
3. **Safety Theatre vs Real Risk** - Systems that look good but don't prevent harm
4. **Professional Burnout and Career Exits** - Deep emotional exhaustion, many planning exits
5. **Credibility Erosion** - Seen as police, not partners
6. **Systems That Look Good, But Perform Poorly** - Burned by previous solutions (Enablon, INX)
7. **Controls That Satisfy Audits But Don't Prevent Harm** - Moral injury from superficial controls

**Evidence:**
- Multiple high-frequency themes with strong emotional intensity
- Quotes show deep frustration: "This job is soul sucking", "I've been banging my head against the wall"
- Strong resentment toward "paper safety" and audit-driven work

**Why it matters:** They need systems that give them leverage, credibility, and impact - not more administrative burden. Tools must: make fatal risk visible to leadership, connect data to action, focus on what matters, make them effective, position them as enablers, and actually work in reality.

### Executive Persona - The Leadership SIF Prevention Paradox

**What we learned:** Executives are not choosing safety vs productivity - they're balancing low-frequency, high-consequence, abstract future harm with immediate, visible, measurable production losses. They lack credible signals of rising SIF risk and default to what they can see and measure.

**Evidence:**
- "Most organisations unintentionally optimise for productivity because they cannot see fatal-risk degradation in time"
- Executives see: missed targets, cost overruns, delays
- Executives don't see: weakening critical controls, repeated near-miss patterns, control failures masked by workarounds
- Decision frame becomes: "We're behind schedule, and I don't see a clear safety reason to stop"

**Why it matters:** Executives need a system that makes SIF risk as visible and relevant as production risk. Dashboards that stay green are dangerous because they create false confidence while fatal risk exposure remains unchanged.

**Recommended Messaging:**
- "Most organisations unintentionally optimise for productivity because they cannot see fatal-risk degradation in time."
- "We make those risks visible early enough to govern them without stopping the business unnecessarily."

### Market vs Customer VoC - The Transformation Evidence

**What we learned:** The market VoC (pre-Forwood) is dominated by scepticism, defensiveness, fatigue, and learned distrust. The customer VoC (post-Forwood) is dominated by clarity, alignment, confidence, and long-term thinking. This contrast demonstrates the transformation Forwood enables.

**Evidence:**

**Market VoC (Pre-Forwood):**
- Scepticism about safety systems protecting companies, not workers
- Defensiveness from accountability without authority
- Fatigue from drowning in data with no action
- Learned distrust from reporting that goes into black holes

**Customer VoC (Post-Forwood):**
- Clarity: "we've kind of flipped the paradigm upside down... now we're really focused on things that can actually really kill you"
- Alignment: "same language, behaviour and engagement across the different levels"
- Confidence: "getting that data in one place and getting it out and making it useful, is the advantage"
- Long-term thinking: "the biggest bang for buck is on the planning side... it pays for itself"

**Why it matters:** This transformation evidence is powerful for sales and marketing. Customers are not naïve or uncritical - they've experienced real change. The absence of cynicism in customer language is meaningful: trust is no longer the dominant concern.

### Persona Psychology - What They Actually Need

**What we learned:** Each persona has distinct jobs-to-be-done, pain points, motivators, and dream states that reveal what they actually need (not what they say they need).

**Evidence:**

**Safety System Owner (Primary):**
- **Jobs To Be Done:** Build and run a safety system that actually controls fatal risks in real work, not just on paper
- **Core Pain Points:** Accountability without authority, Safety theatre vs real risk, Signal lost in noise, Credibility erosion, Burnout
- **Core Motivators:** Preventing irreversible harm, Systemic control not heroics, Credibility and consistency, Leverage with leadership
- **Dream State:** Fatal risk visible and prioritized, Safety survives production pressure, Data drives action not reports, Professional sustainability

**Accountable Executive (Co-Buyer):**
- **Jobs To Be Done:** Ensure the organization can reliably prevent fatal incidents and demonstrate due diligence when it matters
- **Core Pain Points:** False confidence from "green dashboards", Personal exposure they cannot delegate, Lack of line-of-sight from boardroom to field
- **Core Motivators:** Preventing irreversible harm on their watch, Protecting personal and organizational legitimacy, Confidence not optimism (evidence-based)
- **Dream State:** Board-level confidence in fatal-risk control, Small number of meaningful signals, Direct evidence controls work under pressure

**Operational Leader (Adopter):**
- **Jobs To Be Done:** Execute work safely while meeting production/schedule commitments
- **Core Pain Points:** Conflicting incentives (safety vs production), Unclear priorities - too many rules, Being set up to fail
- **Core Motivators:** Practical clarity on what matters most, Legitimacy with crews, Respect from workforce
- **Dream State:** Focus on critical risks (less noise), Clear permission to stop work, Engaged workforce that speaks up

**Why it matters:** Understanding persona psychology enables targeted messaging, product design, and sales conversations that address what they actually need, not generic "safety improvement" language.

### Customer Transformation - Before and After

**What we learned:** Customers experience measurable transformation across eight dimensions after using Forwood, providing concrete evidence of impact.

**Evidence - The Forwood Transformation:**

| Before (Market/EHS Reality) | After (What CRM+ Changed) |
|----------------------------|---------------------------|
| Leadership absent from field | Leaders regularly in field engaging on controls |
| Blame-driven → reporting suppressed | Non-blame → employees opening up |
| Hazard-counting and minor injuries | Fatal-risk focus - "what could kill you" |
| Stop-work theoretical/punished | Stop-work authority real and celebrated |
| Issues hidden or ignored | Issues celebrated and discussed openly |
| Data overload, no insight | Data centralized and actionable |
| Inconsistent language/approach | Consistent language across all levels |
| Individual blame for failures | System-level analysis and improvement |

**Customer Quotes:**
- "One of the key impacts we've seen is, having leaders out in the field on a more regular basis"
- "Because it's a system that does not involved with blame... employees opening up"
- "Celebrating the red, celebrating the issues, instead of it being a bad thing"
- "getting that data in one place and getting it out and making it useful, is the advantage"

**Why it matters:** This transformation evidence demonstrates Forwood's impact and should be used in sales conversations, marketing materials, and customer success stories.

### Messaging Insights - What Resonates

**What we learned:** Specific messaging recommendations for each persona based on their psychology and pain points.

**Evidence:**

**For Primary Persona (Safety System Owner):**
- Lead with fatality prevention (not general safety metrics)
- Address the authority gap - show how tools give leverage with leadership
- Prove it works in reality - not just on paper or in demos
- Position as enabler, not cop - systems that help workers, not catch them
- Acknowledge failed systems (Enablon, INX) and prove this is different

**For Executive Persona:**
- "Most organisations unintentionally optimise for productivity because they cannot see fatal-risk degradation in time"
- "We make those risks visible early enough to govern them without stopping the business unnecessarily"
- Focus on governance obligation and personal accountability
- Show how to make SIF risk as visible as production risk

**For Operational Leader:**
- Focus on practical clarity on what matters most
- Emphasize permission to stop work
- Show how to engage workforce that speaks up

**Why it matters:** Messaging that resonates with persona psychology will be more effective in demand gen, marketing, and sales conversations.

## Implications

### Product Development Implications

**High Priority:**
- **Fatality prevention must be the organizing principle** - not "zero harm" or general safety improvement. Product features should explicitly focus on fatal risk identification and prevention.
- **Data must connect to action** - dashboards and reports are meaningless without visible follow-through. Product must show clear connection between data and actions taken.
- **Make fatal risk visible to leadership** - tools that surface fatal risk to executives in real-time, not just lagging indicators. This addresses the authority gap for EHS professionals.
- **Systems thinking over individual blame** - design for human fallibility, not perfect compliance. Non-blame culture features that reward speaking up.

**Medium Priority:**
- **Credibility is fragile** - one tolerated unsafe act or broken promise undoes progress. Product must support consistent leadership behavior and visible follow-through.
- **Leadership visibility matters** - executives need tools that make SIF risk as visible as production risk. Executive dashboards must show fatal risk signals, not just safety metrics.

### Strategic Implications

**High Priority:**
- **Positioning:** Lead with fatality prevention as the organizing principle, not general safety improvement
- **Messaging:** Use executive messaging - "Most organisations unintentionally optimise for productivity because they cannot see fatal-risk degradation in time"
- **Differentiation:** Acknowledge failed systems (Enablon, INX) and prove Forwood is different - actually works in the field

**Medium Priority:**
- **Sales conversations:** Use persona psychology to address what they actually need, not generic "safety improvement" language
- **Customer success:** Use transformation evidence (before/after) in sales and marketing materials

### Market Implications

**High Priority:**
- **Market sentiment:** The market is deeply sceptical of safety systems. Need to prove Forwood is different - not another layer of bureaucracy.
- **Competitive positioning:** Market hates Enablon, INX - opportunity to differentiate by proving tools actually work in the field

**Medium Priority:**
- **Trust deficit:** Market has learned distrust from reporting that goes into black holes. Product must show visible action and follow-through.

### Customer Implications

**High Priority:**
- **Customer transformation:** Use before/after evidence in sales conversations and marketing materials
- **Customer success stories:** Document and share transformation evidence (leadership in field, non-blame culture, stop-work authority, etc.)

**Medium Priority:**
- **Customer psychology:** Understand that customers are trying to prevent catastrophic events in systems they don't fully control - this is the real problem to address
