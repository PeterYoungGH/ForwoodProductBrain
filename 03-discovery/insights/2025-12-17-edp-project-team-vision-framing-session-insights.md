## Insight Statement

EDP's trust and adoption are at risk due to ongoing Safety+ stability, usability, and support experience issues, requiring a two-track approach: immediate stabilization through quality releases and accelerated migration to Forwood One as the structural solution. The 3-hour executive session revealed critical operational dependencies (mid-January deadline), support process friction, and higher-order requirements (taxonomy, data integrity, reporting actionability) that must be addressed to ensure successful migration and rebuild trust.

---

## Evidence

### Trust and Adoption Risk is Acute

**Critical Issues:**
- High contractor population and limited face-to-face engagement; tool failures create "noise" that escalates through contractor management chains
- Field users (including non-technical workers) resist "questionnaires" and evidence capture; when the app fails, adoption regresses to paper
- Need for the tool to be stable and "fully running" to start the year at full pace (mid-January emphasis)
- Forwood explicitly acknowledged under-delivery versus expectations and the impact on trust (including recognising "angry/frustrated" sentiment)

**Implication:** Stability is blocking adoption - users are reverting to paper when tool fails. Contractor sentiment is trending negatively and needs rapid stabilization. Mid-January deadline is critical for EDP operations.

### Support Process Friction is Material

**EDP Experience:**
- Repeated requests for screenshots/videos and "cannot reproduce" responses
- Time burden is material (high daily email volume)
- EDP wants a more efficient diagnostic process and clearer, faster answers on:
  - Is it known?
  - Is it a defect?
  - When will it be fixed?

**Forwood Commitment:**
- Acknowledged the described support loop is unacceptable
- Committed to review internal support process and expectations for evidence capture to reduce back-and-forth
- Action: Review support evidence-gathering process and propose streamlined diagnostic checklist (Jan 2026)

**Implication:** Support loop is a material pain point that must be addressed to rebuild trust and reduce customer frustration.

### Reporting Actionability is Critical

**EDP Requirements:**
- EDP has high data volumes but cannot reliably derive clear actions
- Some inconsistencies reported between Safety+ and dashboards
- Difficulty producing "printable" verification records (PDF-like) with responses and signatures for sharing with contractors
- Need for action-oriented dashboards that enable immediate action based on results

**Forwood Response:**
- Proposed starting dashboard improvement work immediately (via QuickSight)
- Forwood to bring a reporting specialist onto a call early in the new year
- PDF-style output is available in Forwood One

**Implication:** Reporting improvements must start immediately to enable EDP to take clear actions from data. This is critical for demonstrating value and rebuilding trust.

### Higher-Order Requirements Need Strategic Planning

**Taxonomy Complexity:**
- Contractor–subcontractor hierarchies, multi-country structures, and company mappings described as clunky in current Safety+
- EDP reiterated contractor/subcontractor hierarchy is central to their operating model
- Forwood indicated Forwood One taxonomy is a significant redesign enabled by new technology and should better support complex structures
- Forwood will not commit to "fixing taxonomy" in existing Safety+ due to platform limitations

**Data Integrity and Veracity:**
- EDP sees a meaningful portion of submissions as unreliable (described as users rushing or intentionally selecting "pass" answers to obtain approval to work)
- Desire for additional barriers or intelligence to detect suspicious patterns and focus supervision/spot checks where the risk is highest
- EDP requested a way to correlate worker submissions with supervisor checks to enable actioning
- Forwood agreed to explore short-term reporting-based approaches and put "correlated cross-check" concept into Product Management roadmap discussion

**Implication:** Higher-order asks (taxonomy flexibility, evidence rules, data integrity controls, and correlated cross-checking) will be worked through in January roadmap and February transition workshops. These are strategic requirements that Forwood One must address.

### Two-Track Approach to Rebuild Trust

**Immediate Stabilization:**
- 18 Dec release (target) to address multiple high-priority items with quality gate emphasis
- Items confirmed: Usernames alphabetical order, offline verifications upload issue resolved, contractor company mismatch resolved, records appearing in "recent verifications" resolved
- Reporting improvements to start immediately

**Accelerated Forwood One Pathway:**
- January 2026: Roadmap session with Product Management
- February 2026: Detailed transition workshops
- March 2026: Migration execution activities begin
- Early April 2026 (target): Migrate EDP to Forwood One

**Implication:** Success depends on clean release execution, visible reduction in high-impact defects, and fast delivery of reporting improvements. Forwood One migration is strategic and requires careful planning and execution.

## Context

This was a critical 3-hour executive vision framing session with EDP to reset expectations and rebuild trust following ongoing Safety+ stability, usability, and support experience issues. The session resulted in:

- Clear acknowledgement of delivery gaps and trust impact
- Commitment to two-track approach: immediate stabilisation + accelerated Forwood One pathway
- Specific near-term delivery commitments (18 Dec release, reporting improvements)
- Detailed transition plan for Forwood One migration (target early April 2026)
- Multiple action items with owners and timelines

The session captured both immediate operational concerns and strategic vision for Forwood One migration, providing a comprehensive view of EDP's requirements and expectations.

## Implications

### Product

**High Priority:**
- **Release quality gates** must be enforced - 18 Dec release must be clean and address high-priority items without regressions
- **Reporting improvements** must start immediately - Dashboard improvements are critical for EDP to take clear actions from data
- **Support process redesign** - Streamlined diagnostic process needed to reduce back-and-forth and improve customer experience
- **Taxonomy redesign** - Forwood One must better support contractor/subcontractor hierarchies and multi-country structures
- **Data integrity mechanisms** - Need mechanisms to detect suspicious patterns and improve veracity of user submissions

**Medium Priority:**
- **Mobile app continuity** - Clear communication about app functionality during and after migration
- **PDF output capabilities** - Ensure printable verification records are available in Forwood One
- **Correlation reporting** - Explore short-term reporting-based approaches for cross-checking verifications

### Strategy

**High Priority:**
- **Forwood One migration is strategic** - Early April 2026 target requires careful planning and execution
- **Trust rebuilding is critical** - Success depends on clean release execution, visible reduction in high-impact defects, and fast delivery of reporting improvements
- **Two-track approach** - Balance immediate stabilization with strategic migration planning

**Medium Priority:**
- **Roadmap alignment** - January roadmap session must map EDP outcomes to Forwood One capabilities
- **Transition planning** - February transition workshops must address scope, cutover, security, and readiness

### Operations

**High Priority:**
- **Support process improvement** - Review and improve support evidence-gathering process (Jan 2026)
- **Reporting specialist engagement** - Schedule reporting-focused session early January 2026
- **Product Management roadmap session** - Schedule January 2026 to map desired outcomes to Forwood One capabilities

**Medium Priority:**
- **Correlation reporting investigation** - Explore feasibility using time/location/role heuristics (Jan–Feb 2026)
- **Transition workshop planning** - Define transition workshop plan for February 2026
- **Migration execution prep** - Begin migration execution prep in March 2026
