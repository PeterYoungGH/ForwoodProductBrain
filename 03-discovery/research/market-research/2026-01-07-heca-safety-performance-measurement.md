## Key Findings

### Finding 1: TRIR Limitations

**Statistical Limitations:**
- TRIR is not statistically valid for short-term or comparative use
- Analysis of trillions of worker hours shows injuries are largely random events
- Requires extremely large datasets to be statistically meaningful
- Subject to underreporting and manipulation

**Philosophical Limitations:**
- TRIR reflects an outdated view of safety as the absence of injury, rather than the presence of safeguards
- Does not focus on SIF (Serious Injury and Fatality) prevention
- Does not predict future injuries or fatalities
- No demonstrated relationship with SIFs
- Does not support proactive or targeted intervention

**Conclusion:** While TRIR is simple and familiar, it is fundamentally misaligned with contemporary safety thinking.

### Finding 2: HECA Methodology

**Definition:**
HECA (High-Energy Control Assessment) is defined as the **percentage of high-energy hazards with a corresponding direct control**.

**Formula:**
HECA = Successes / (Successes + Exposures)

Where:
- **Success:** A high-energy hazard has a corresponding direct control
- **Exposure:** A high-energy hazard does not have a corresponding direct control

**High-Energy Hazards:**
- Defined as hazards involving more than **1,500 joules of physical energy**
- Most likely to result in serious injury or fatality
- Examples: Suspended loads, mobile equipment, heavy rotating equipment, electrical energy (>50V), pressure and explosions, high-temperature sources

**Direct Controls:**
Must meet all three criteria:
1. **Targeted** to the specific high-energy hazard
2. **Effectively mitigates or eliminates** the hazard energy to below the SIF threshold when properly installed, verified, and used
3. **Effective despite unintentional human error** during work execution

Examples: Engineered fall protection, machine guarding, lockout/tagout, trench boxes, de-energised electrical systems

Controls reliant on perfect human behaviour (e.g. signage, training alone, situational awareness) are not considered direct controls.

### Finding 3: HECA as Monitoring Variable

HECA is positioned between traditional leading and lagging indicators:
- **Not a leading indicator:** Does not measure activities
- **Not a lagging indicator:** Does not rely on incidents
- **Monitoring variable:** Enables near real-time insight into safety system effectiveness before serious incidents occur

### Finding 4: HECA Evaluation Against Metric Criteria

**Objective:** High potential, though some observer judgement remains

**Valid:** Can be collected in high volume, enabling statistical stability

**Predictive:** Not yet empirically proven

**Clear:** Moderately clear; requires training

**Important:** Strong alignment with modern safety principles and SIF prevention

**Actionable:** Supports proactive decision-making and capacity building

### Finding 5: Operational Considerations

**Data Capture Requirements:**
- High-energy hazards observed
- Presence or absence of direct controls
- Types of direct controls present
- Controls missing where exposure exists

**Analysis Capabilities:**
- Trend analysis over time
- Comparison across business units
- Identification of poorly controlled hazard categories
- Targeted investment in missing controls
- Organisational learning and resource allocation

**Visualisations:** Can show trends by hazard type, control type, location, or business unit

### Finding 6: Adoption Recommendations

For successful HECA adoption:
- A single, shared definition of HECA must be maintained
- HECA should be used for learning and improvement, not comparison or incentives
- Operational guidance, sampling strategies, and validation are required
- Relationships between leading indicators, HECA, and lagging indicators should be empirically explored

## Insights

Synthesized insights from the research:

- **Modern Safety Science Shift:** The research reflects a shift from measuring safety as "absence of injury" (TRIR) to measuring safety as "presence of safeguards" (HECA), aligning with contemporary safety thinking
- **Energy-Based Classification:** Focusing on high-energy hazards (>1,500 joules) shifts attention from worst-case possibilities to most likely severe outcomes, improving prioritisation and SIF prevention
- **Direct Controls Emphasis:** The methodology emphasizes controls that are effective despite human error, moving away from reliance on perfect human behaviour
- **Monitoring vs Leading/Lagging:** HECA positions itself as a "monitoring variable" rather than traditional leading/lagging indicators, enabling near real-time insight
- **Statistical Validity:** HECA addresses TRIR's statistical limitations by enabling high-volume data collection for statistical stability
