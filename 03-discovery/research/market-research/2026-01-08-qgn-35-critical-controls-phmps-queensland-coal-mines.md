## Research Question

What are the regulatory requirements for integrating critical controls into Principal Hazard Management Plans (PHMPs) for Queensland coal mines, and what process must mines follow to achieve compliance?

## Key Findings

### Finding 1: Mandatory Compliance Deadline

**Legislative Requirements:**
- Amendments to the Coal Mining Safety and Health Act 1999 (CMSHA) through the Resources Safety and Health Legislation Amendment Act 2024
- Provisions relating to critical controls commence on 1 June 2025
- **One-year transition period: Mines must comply by 1 June 2026**
- Applies to Principal Hazards where threshold for Material Unwanted Event (MUE) is multiple fatalities (more than one fatality)
- If a Coal Mine Operator (CMO) determines a single fatality to be the MUE threshold, this guidance may still be adopted

**Regulatory Context:**
- Findings from Queensland Coal Mining Board of Inquiry (2020) demonstrated that controls for incidents with potential for multiple fatalities have not been effectively implemented or maintained
- This resulted in mandatory legislative amendments requiring critical controls

### Finding 2: Nine-Step Integration Process

The guidance outlines a comprehensive 9-step process for integrating critical controls into PHMPs:

1. **Planning** - Site Senior Executive (SSE) develops documented plan covering organizational context, scope, objectives, timelines, MUE threshold, SHMS amendments, consultation, resources, responsibilities, competency requirements, and tools
2. **Identify all Principal Hazards and MUEs** - Typically using Broad-Brush Risk Assessment (BBRA)
3. **Identify controls** - Issue-specific risk assessments using Bowtie Analysis or other recognized methodologies
4. **Select the critical controls** - Those necessary to prevent an MUE or mitigate its consequences
5. **Define critical control performance** - Record in PHMP using Critical Control Information Summary Sheet with 10 required elements
6. **Accountability and assigning responsibility** - Document roles and responsibilities, MUE ownership, auditing, monitoring, effectiveness review, and reporting
7. **Verification** - Workplace monitoring (by CMWs and supervisors) and critical control auditing (structured evaluation program)
8. **Critical control reporting and recording** - Results must be recorded, reported, reviewed by MUE Owner, Officers, CMO, and SSE
9. **Response to inadequate critical control performance** - Must initiate response to protect workers until control is restored, may require withdrawal to place of safety under s273 CMSHA

**Process Characteristics:**
- Iterative process that may require revisiting earlier steps
- Based on ICMM (International Council on Mining and Metals) guidance documents
- Aligned with CMSHA requirements and Queensland coal mining industry

### Finding 3: Critical Control Information Requirements

**Required Documentation Elements:**
Each critical control must be documented in PHMP using a Critical Control Information Summary Sheet (or similar) including:

1. Relevant Principal Hazard(s) or MUE(s)
2. MUE owner
3. Name of the critical control
4. Objectives of the critical control
5. Critical control performance requirements
6. Support activities
7. Verification activities
8. Responsible role and verification frequency
9. Target performance
10. Performance trigger (indicates when workplace becomes dangerous for purposes of s273 CMSHA)

**Verification Requirements:**
- Workplace monitoring: Conducted by Coal Mine Workers (CMWs) and supervisors before and during work
- Critical control auditing: Structured evaluation program established by SSE
- Audit outcomes must be reviewed by Officers, CMO, and SSE to ensure risks remain acceptable and ALARA (As Low As Reasonably Achievable)

### Finding 4: Accountability and Responsibility Framework

**Key Roles:**
- **Site Senior Executive (SSE):** Responsible for developing and implementing the Safety and Health Management System (SHMS), including PHMPs and critical controls
- **MUE Owner:** Responsible for reviewing and approving audits, with corrective actions documented
- **Coal Mine Workers (CMWs):** Conduct workplace monitoring before and during work
- **Supervisors:** Conduct workplace monitoring
- **Officers, CMO, SSE:** Must review audit outcomes to ensure risks remain acceptable and ALARA

**Management Structures:**
- Must align with Recognised Standard 22
- Roles and responsibilities must be documented including MUE ownership, auditing, monitoring, effectiveness review, and reporting

### Finding 5: Response to Inadequate Performance

**Requirements:**
- If a critical control does not meet target performance, a response must be initiated to protect CMWs until the control is restored
- Responses must be approved and documented
- May require withdrawal to a place of safety under s273 CMSHA
- Communication to affected CMWs must enable informed decision-making

## Insights

Synthesized insights from the research:

1. **Time-Sensitive Compliance Requirement:** Queensland coal mines have a hard deadline of June 1, 2026 to integrate critical controls into PHMPs. This creates an urgent need for tools and processes that support this integration.

2. **Comprehensive Documentation Requirements:** The 10-element Critical Control Information Summary Sheet requirement means mines need structured documentation capabilities. This could be a product opportunity for Forwood's platform.

3. **Verification and Monitoring Workflow:** The dual requirement for workplace monitoring (by workers/supervisors) and structured auditing (by SSE) suggests a need for workflow tools that support both operational monitoring and formal audit processes.

4. **Accountability Chain:** The clear accountability framework (SSE → MUE Owner → Officers/CMO) suggests a need for role-based access and approval workflows in any supporting software.

5. **Performance Trigger Management:** The requirement for "performance triggers" that indicate when the workplace becomes dangerous suggests a need for real-time monitoring and alerting capabilities.

6. **Regulatory Alignment:** This guidance is based on ICMM best practices but adapted for Queensland legislation, suggesting Forwood's platform should support both international best practices and jurisdiction-specific requirements.

## Implications

What do these findings mean for product decisions?
