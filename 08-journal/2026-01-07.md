## Entry

### UAT Progress

Three UAT interviews have been completed to date.

The first two sessions were stopped early due to major platform issues that prevented meaningful progress.

The third session was completed largely intact, without critical "game-ender" failures, however significant usability and quality issues were still identified.

Based on today's discussion, the decision was made to continue UAT rather than pause or stop it.

UAT will continue with additional participants, including Angelina and Steve.

While UAT is proceeding, there are ongoing concerns about platform quality, particularly usability, which are being identified and tracked as part of the UAT process.

### Engineering Release Readiness

The engineering team has now completed a full spreadsheet assessment of features against technical release criteria.

**Findings:**

- 9 features have not met technical release completion criteria.
- 4–5 features have met criteria.
- 1 feature does not yet have a rating.

This equates to approximately a 30–35% completion rate against release standards.

Engineering is actively working to address the gaps.

### Context and Impact

Engineering communicated to the broader business that technical release was complete on 23 December.

As of 7 January, significant work remains outstanding.

This has had a direct impact on Product, including:

- Inability to conduct a clean and effective UAT.
- Inability to run clean QA on other areas of the platform.
- Flow-on impacts to other assessment activities (e.g. WED's independent reviews and other system areas outside UAT).
- The ongoing instability and remediation work is consuming substantial team time and attention.

### Discussion with Warwick and Ashley

In today's meeting with Warwick and Ashley, I raised that due to:

- The severity of the outstanding issues, and
- The time being spent resolving UAT and quality feedback,

the planned V2 scope completion work cannot proceed this week.

Warwick agreed with this assessment.

This will push timelines for downstream activities, including EDP.

While agreement was given, I deliberately framed this as a consequential issue:

- Technical release was not completed when stated.
- This has directly delayed UAT, quality assurance, and the planning and detailed scoping of subsequent features.
- This is something the engineering team will need to formally carry forward and own in terms of impact and recovery planning.

### Personal Reflection

This note is intended as a factual record of events and decisions.

While next steps are still forming, it is important that the timing mismatch between stated release completion and actual readiness is clearly documented, along with its downstream consequences.
